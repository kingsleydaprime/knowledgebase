# Mathematics

The math that underpins machine learning and deep learning — shared by the [[ai-ml/01-data-scientist/index|Data Scientist]] and [[ai-ml/02-ml-engineer/index|ML Engineer]] paths (the [[ai-ml/03-ai-engineer/index|AI Engineer]] path needs far less of it). Part of [[ai-ml/00-foundations/index|00-foundations]].

Each area is deep enough to be its own folder of atomic notes rather than one flat file.

## Reading order

1. [[ai-ml/00-foundations/03-mathematics/01-linear-algebra/index|linear-algebra/]] — **[Beginner → Intermediate]** — vectors, matrices, dot products, matrix multiplication — why weights and embeddings are what they are
2. [[ai-ml/00-foundations/03-mathematics/02-calculus/index|calculus/]] — **[Intermediate]** — derivatives, gradients, the chain rule — why backpropagation works
3. [[ai-ml/00-foundations/03-mathematics/03-probability-and-statistics/index|probability-and-statistics/]] — **[Beginner → Intermediate]** — distributions, expectation/variance, Bayes — why loss functions and sampling look the way they do
4. [[ai-ml/00-foundations/03-mathematics/04-optimization|optimization]] — **[Intermediate]** — gradient descent, learning rate, batches — how training actually runs

## Gaps to fill later (per roadmap.sh)

The Data Scientist and ML Engineer roadmaps expect more than the above: **more linear algebra** (eigenvalues/eigenvectors, SVD, determinants & matrix inverse), **inferential statistics** (hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, distribution types) beyond the descriptive basics, and **discrete math**. These are noted for a later pass.
