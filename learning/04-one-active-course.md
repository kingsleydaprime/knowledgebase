# 04 · One Active Course

> [Beginner] · Many interests. One at a time.

## The rule

**One active course. Everything else waits its turn.**

Active: **SWE 101.**

Catalogue (real, not abandoned): Robotics 101 · Music 101 · Art 101 · Finance 101 · Public Speaking 101 · Manhood 101 · Podcasting 101 · Ministry · Travel · Reading.

The live register is [[learning/catalogue|catalogue]].

## The actual problem

The problem is not having many interests. The problem is that my brain reads *every interesting possibility as something requiring immediate action.*

Mid-study, the intrusions sound like:

- "Should I be learning something else?"
- "My classmate is doing research; maybe I should be doing research."
- "Someone else is improving physically; maybe I should focus on that."
- "I could also learn music / art / robotics."

Which produces a permanent low-grade sense of being behind, while actually being ahead of where I was.

## The two phrases

> **Not now does not mean never.**

> **Kingsley, it's fine. One step at a time.**

## The protocol when something interesting appears

**Notice it → write it down → park it → return to the current priority.**

Writing it down is not a formality. It's what makes parking survivable — the idea is captured, so it doesn't have to keep shouting to avoid being lost. Park it in [[learning/catalogue|catalogue]].

The pause to insert before acting:

> **"Interesting. Do I need to act on this now?"**

Almost always: no.

## Curiosity vs comparison

Same observation, two different engines. Learn to tell them apart:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Healthy — curiosity** | "I wonder what this person is doing differently." |
| **Unhealthy — comparison** | "I'm behind because this person can do something I can't." |

Things worth repeating to myself:

- I don't need to solve future problems today.
- I don't need to become every impressive person I see.
- **Someone else's achievement is not automatically my assignment.**
- Feeling like I should be doing more doesn't mean I should.
- I don't need to become every impressive person. I need to become myself at a high level.

## Why software engineering is the active one

Current season: **foundation-building.** Priorities, roughly in order:

1. Spiritual life
2. Income / career
3. Character and confidence
4. Attention / discipline
5. Health
6. Everything else

The near-term mission is financial capability through software engineering — DSA, engineering study, applications, interview prep, projects, technical depth, employability.

The point isn't money. The point is:

> **Build enough financial freedom to eventually explore the rest of life.**

Travel, creative work, robotics, music, art, business, learning, experiences, family responsibilities — all of it later gets funded by this.

**So focusing on SWE now is not abandoning the other interests. It's building their funding mechanism.** That reframe is the whole reason the rule is tolerable.

## The long view

The interests aren't random. They cluster:

- **Builder** — software, engineering, robotics, business
- **Creator** — art, drawing, music, podcasting
- **Communicator** — public speaking, podcasting, teaching, ministry
- **Explorer** — travel, new experiences, learning about the world

Common thread: **curiosity + observation + learning + building + communication.**

I don't have to pick one identity for life. Different seasons emphasise different parts. The goal is to become capable enough to eventually hold several of these in one life — **not to run them all simultaneously now.**
