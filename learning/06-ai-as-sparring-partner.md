# 06 · AI as Sparring Partner

> [Beginner] · The rule that keeps the rest of the system from being hollowed out.

## The risk

Modern AI can make learning completely passive. Ask, receive, feel informed, retain nothing. It short-circuits exactly the steps that do the work in [[learning/02-the-learning-loop|the loop]] — reconstruction, practice, and checking.

The failure mode is subtle because the output *looks* like the synthesis step. A clean explanation handed to me is not the same artefact as a clean explanation I wrote after reconstructing the material. They're indistinguishable on the page and completely different in my head.

## The rule

> **I stay the thinker. AI is the sparring partner.**

When I'm learning something, AI should preferably:

- give hints, not answers
- ask me questions
- quiz me
- critique my explanation
- identify gaps in my reasoning
- challenge my assumptions
- review a solution I already attempted
- help me debug my *thinking*, not just my code

And should push me to attempt the hard problem first.

This matters most for **DSA and technical fundamentals**, where the whole point is the reps. It matters least when I'm shipping something under time pressure and already know the material — that's a tool-use situation, not a learning situation.

## The two modes, named

| Mode | When | AI behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Sparring** | I'm learning. DSA, a new domain, anything in an active course. | Hints, questions, critique, quizzes. No worked solution until I've attempted one. |
| **Tooling** | I'm building or shipping something I already understand. | Normal. Do the work, explain the choices. |

I should say which mode I'm in. If I don't, and it's clearly study, default to **sparring**.

## The tell that I'm in the wrong mode

If I've read three good explanations and still can't reconstruct the idea on a blank board — I've been consuming, not learning. Close everything and go to step 3 of the loop.
