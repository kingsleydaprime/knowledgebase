## The one-page version

I'm curious about a lot of things and I study them rather than just consume them. That's a strength. The failure mode is that my brain treats every interesting possibility as requiring immediate action, which produces a constant feeling of being behind.

So: **one active course at a time**, everything else captured and parked. Learning happens on paper and on the board through reconstruction and practice, not by rereading. The laptop comes last, for synthesis. AI gets to spar, not to answer. And stillness is part of the system, not a gap in it.

The proof that this works isn't theoretical — a structured syllabus, a board, one subject per day and a measurable target got me 328 in JAMB at 15, and produced a physics textbook out of the notes afterwards.

## The twenty principles

1. **Curiosity is a strength.**
2. **One thing at a time.**
3. **Not now does not mean never.**
4. **Don't turn every interest into an emergency.**
5. **Learning and doing are different.**
6. **Use physical notes and active recall.**
7. **Use the board as a thinking arena.**
8. **Use digital notes for synthesis and reference.**
9. **Practice problems instead of only consuming explanations.**
10. **Let AI challenge thinking rather than replace it.**
11. **Give vague ambitions a structure through personal courses.**
12. **Use measurable targets and visible progress.**
13. **Protect boredom and stillness.**
14. **Distinguish curiosity from comparison.**
15. **Don't mistake rest for wasted time.**
16. **Don't solve tomorrow's problems today.**
17. **Build the future without refusing to experience the present.**
18. **Keep the joy of learning alive.**
19. **Direct curiosity instead of suppressing it.**
20. **One active course; everything else can wait its turn.**

## The simplest summary

I don't need to become less curious. I need to get better at **directing** curiosity.

I don't need fewer interests forever. I need to **give each interest its season**.

I don't need to become a productivity machine. I need **clear targets, structure, active practice, and enough freedom to enjoy learning.**

And most importantly: **I genuinely like learning. Protect that.**
