## The self-study standard

Use [[COURSE-STANDARD|the shared course standard]] for the teaching notes. It borrows the motivation, plain-language explanations, and visual steps of the data-structures modules, then adds the missing bridge to independent work.

For each module, define:

1. **Starting knowledge and outcomes** — explicit prerequisites, not a promise of zero background; what I will be able to explain and do.
2. **The mechanism** — one concrete problem, terms introduced as needed, a state-by-state worked example, and why the steps work.
3. **The practice** — predict → reconstruct with the example closed → make a guided change → implement or solve a new variation independently.
4. **The feedback** — runnable checks or an assessment rubric, boundary cases, and hints/answers separated from the attempt.
5. **The finish line** — a demonstrable artifact and the next lesson it prepares me for.

Use code, diagrams, proofs, or physical experiments according to the subject. Big-O tables and Python implementations are not requirements for every kind of course. Clearly distinguish runnable code from fragments, and provide setup and safety limits for experiments.

_This template plans my learning. The shared standard governs how the lessons teach it; neither is evidence that I've learned the subject yet._
