# 01 · How I Learn

> [Beginner] · The profile. What kind of learner I actually am, and the evidence for it.

## The pattern underneath everything

I don't just consume things. I study them.

When I watch someone act, I study their movements. When someone has social presence, I want to know why people gravitate toward them. Watching football, I notice *how* someone kicks the ball. Hearing music, I notice what's being played.

The loop is always the same:

**Observe → Question → Understand → Experiment**

It shows up in software, engineering, robotics, art, music, sports, public speaking, business, and in watching people. It is not distraction. It's a cognitive style, and it's a strength — the job is to **direct** it, not to eliminate it.

## I actually enjoy this

Not for status. Not for productivity. I enjoy the moment a thing clicks and becomes understandable — enough that I sometimes laugh to myself while learning.

That matters practically: **I don't usually need more external motivation. I need better structure.** Anything that turns learning into a productivity contest works against the thing that already works.

> The identity line: *"I like figuring things out."* Protect that.

## The evidence: JAMB, age 15

Target: above 300. Result: **328**.

The score isn't the point. The *environment* is the point. What I had:

- a clear, measurable target
- a defined syllabus
- a structured schedule — specific subjects on specific days
- repeated practice and problem-solving
- a visible board
- limited distractions
- a strong sense of purpose
- and eventually, genuine enjoyment

The schedule looked like: Monday Maths · Tuesday Physics · Wednesday Chemistry · Thursday Maths · Friday another subject · Saturday English.

**I did not try to master everything every day. Each subject had its turn.** That is the whole model, and it still applies.

## The second piece of evidence

During the COVID/strike period after JAMB, I ended up writing what was essentially a physics textbook out of my accumulated notes.

That's the full chain, already demonstrated once:

**Learning → Notes → Understanding → Organization → Synthesis → Creation**

Worth remembering on any day it feels like I've lost the ability to focus or learn. The ability isn't theoretical. It's on the record.

## On flow

I remember an unusually strong flow state during JAMB prep and I haven't hit it the same way since.

The wrong reading: *"I lost my ability to focus."*

The right reading: *"I previously had an environment with a clear target, structure, repetition, feedback, and limited distractions."*

So flow isn't the goal to chase directly. The conditions are:

**Clear target + visible structure + measurable progress + active problem solving + reduced distraction + meaningful challenge.**

Rebuild those and the state follows.
