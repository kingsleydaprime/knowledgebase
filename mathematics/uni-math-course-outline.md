Here is a unified, comprehensive curriculum that bridges a University Mathematics (B.Sc.) major with a University Engineering Mathematics track.
In standard universities, Mathematics majors focus heavily on theoretical proofs and structural abstraction (like Real Analysis and Abstract Algebra), while Engineers focus on calculations, modeling, and physical applications (like Numerical Methods and Transform Theory). Combining them creates a highly rigorous Mathematical Engineering / Applied Mathematics curriculum.

---

## 🏛 Year 1: Foundations of Higher Mathematics & Calculus

Focus: Transitioning from secondary school calculation to university-level abstraction and foundational tools.

## First Semester

- Calculus I (Single-Variable): Limits, continuity, formal definition of derivative, differentiation techniques, optimization, Mean Value Theorem, Riemann sums, and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
- Algebra & Trigonometry (Foundations): Mathematical induction, complex numbers (De Moivre’s Theorem), theory of quadratic and polynomial equations, binomial theorem, and hyperbolic functions.
- Introduction to Logic & Set Theory: Propositions, truth tables, quantifiers, methods of mathematical proof (direct, contradiction, contrapositive), sets, relations, and functions.

## Second Semester

- Calculus II (Techniques of Integration): Integration by parts, trigonometric substitution, partial fractions, improper integrals, and applications (volumes of revolution, arc length).
- Vectors & Analytic Geometry: Vector algebra in 2D and 3D, dot and cross products, lines and planes in space, and quadric surfaces.
- Introductory Linear Algebra: Systems of linear equations, Gaussian elimination, matrices, determinants, Cramer's rule, and inverse matrices.

---

## 🏛 Year 2: Advanced Calculus, Linear Systems & Foundations of Analysis

Focus: Expanding to multi-dimensional systems and beginning structural rigor.

## First Semester

- Calculus III (Multivariable Calculus): Partial derivatives, chain rule, directional derivatives, gradients, maxima/minima (Lagrange multipliers), double and triple integrals in cylindrical and spherical coordinates.
- Linear Algebra I: Vector spaces, subspaces, linear independence, bases, dimension, linear transformations, kernel and image, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors.
- Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) I: First-order ODEs (separable, exact, linear), second-order linear homogeneous and non-homogeneous equations (undetermined coefficients, variation of parameters), and engineering applications (R-L-C circuits, mass-spring systems).

## Second Semester

- Vector Calculus: Vector fields, curl and divergence, line and surface integrals, and the fundamental classical theorems (Green's, Stokes', and Divergence/Gauss' Theorems).
- Introduction to Real Analysis: The real number system, topology of $\mathbb{R}$, sequences and series of real numbers, convergence tests, and uniform continuity.
- Mathematical Methods for Engineers: Fourier series, periodic functions, even/odd expansions, and introduction to boundary value problems.

---

## 🏛 Year 3: Transform Theories, Advanced Analysis & Numerical Computation

Focus: Advanced modeling for engineers alongside pure mathematical structures.

## First Semester

- Partial Differential Equations (PDEs): Formulation of physical models, heat equation, wave equation, Laplace’s equation, and separation of variables.
- Complex Analysis: Analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann equations, complex integration, Cauchy’s Integral Theorem, Taylor and Laurent series, residue calculus, and conformal mapping (crucial for fluid dynamics and electromagnetics).
- Abstract Algebra I: Binary operations, groups, subgroups, cyclic groups, permutation groups, Lagrange's theorem, and introductory ring theory.

## Second Semester

- Transform Theory: Laplace Transforms (properties, inverse transforms, convolution, solving ODE systems) and Fourier Transforms (properties, transforms of derivatives, applications to engineering systems).
- Numerical Analysis / Methods: Numerical solutions of non-linear equations (Newton-Raphson), numerical integration (Simpson’s, Trapezoidal rules), numerical solutions of ODEs (Euler, Runge-Kutta methods), and polynomial interpolation.
- Probability & Mathematical Statistics: Probability spaces, random variables, discrete and continuous distributions (Normal, Binomial, Poisson), joint distributions, central limit theorem, and parameter estimation.

---

## 🏛 Year 4: Applied Computation, Optimization & Specialized Synthesis

Focus: High-level specialization, combining proof-based understanding with heavy computational design.

## First Semester

- Numerical Solutions of PDEs: Finite Difference Methods (FDM), introductory Finite Element Methods (FEM), stability and convergence criteria for engineering simulations.
- Optimization Theory & Operational Research: Linear programming (Simplex method), non-linear optimization, duality, Kuhn-Tucker conditions, and dynamic programming.
- Real Analysis II: Lebesgue measure, Lebesgue integration, convergence theorems, and differentiation of integrals.

## Second Semester

- Fluid Dynamics & Continuum Mechanics: Kinematics of fluids, Navier-Stokes equations, inviscid flow, and stress/strain tensors in engineering materials.
- Stochastic Processes & Control Theory: Markov chains, Poisson processes, state-space representation, stability analysis (Lyapunov functions), and feedback control loops.
- Final Year Project / Thesis: A comprehensive research project applying pure mathematical theory (e.g., functional analysis, topology, or advanced abstract geometry) to solve a complex, real-world engineering problem.
