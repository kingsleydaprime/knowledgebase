# The Honest Assessment

**The section most web3 material doesn't have.** Every other folder here explains how things work; this one asks whether they should be used, where the money actually goes, and which criticisms survive scrutiny.

**Read [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/01-what-blockchains-are-actually-good-for|note 01]] early — ideally right after [[web3/01-foundations/README|foundations]], not last.** Its test kills most blockchain proposals in about ten seconds, and knowing it while you learn the rest keeps the whole domain in proportion.

This section is deliberately even-handed in both directions: the field's boosterism and its blanket dismissal are each about half right, and neither is useful for building anything.

## Reading order
1. [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/01-what-blockchains-are-actually-good-for|what-blockchains-are-actually-good-for]] — **[Intermediate]** — **the four-question test**, the six use cases that survive it, the ones that don't, and why it's worth learning anyway
2. [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/02-scams-rugs-and-the-fraud-surface|scams-rugs-and-the-fraud-surface]] — **[Beginner]** — **how people actually lose money, which is mostly not smart contract exploits.** Phishing, rugs, honeypots, pig butchering — and the base rates
3. [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/03-regulation-and-the-legal-layer|regulation-and-the-legal-layer]] — **[Intermediate]** — **decentralisation is not a shield for the people around a protocol.** Mango, Tornado Cash, bZx DAO; MiCA; what it means for building. **Dates fastest of any note here**
4. [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/04-energy-and-the-externalities|energy-and-the-externalities]] — **[Intermediate]** — the numbers, what the Merge actually changed, and **why the surviving externalities aren't environmental**
