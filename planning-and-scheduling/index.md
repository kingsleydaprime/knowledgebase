# Techniques of Planning and Scheduling

## What planning and scheduling is

Planning and scheduling is working out, before a project starts, what has to be done, in what order, how long it will take, what it will cost and who will do it, and then keeping that plan true while the work goes on.

You've done it in small ways. Organising a wedding, you know the hall must be booked before the cards are printed, and the cake ordered a week before the day. Building a fence, you dig the holes before you put up the posts. This subject turns that common sense into methods that still work when there are hundreds of activities, an uncertain future, a tight budget and too few workers.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end you will be able to:
- say what a project is and what surrounds it, and outline how PMBOK organises project management;
- break a project into activities and draw them as a network, both with boxes and with arrows;
- find how long a project takes and which activities can't slip;
- give the chance of finishing by a date when durations are uncertain;
- work out the cheapest way to finish a project sooner;
- fit a plan to the workers and machines you really have;
- choose between projects, draw schedules as charts, and measure progress in money.

## Key ideas to remember

Once you've read the lessons, these are the points worth keeping. Each links to where it's explained.

1. **Scope, time and cost are tied:** change one and another moves. → [[planning-and-scheduling/03-the-triple-constraint|03]]
2. **You can't schedule what you haven't listed;** the WBS lists it. → [[planning-and-scheduling/06-work-breakdown-structure|06]]
3. **Forward pass takes the maximum; backward pass takes the minimum.** → [[planning-and-scheduling/14-critical-path-method-2|14]]
4. **Zero float means critical,** and the critical path is the longest path. → [[planning-and-scheduling/14-critical-path-method-2|14]]
5. **PERT: $t_e = (a + 4m + b)/6$, and add variances along the critical path only.** → [[planning-and-scheduling/18-pert-2|18]]
6. **Crash only critical activities, cheapest slope first, while the slope is below the daily overhead.** → [[planning-and-scheduling/23-crashing-2|23]]
7. **A network with times is not a schedule until resources are assigned,** and limited resources usually lengthen a project. → [[planning-and-scheduling/26-resources-2|26]]
8. **Earned value separates "behind schedule" from "over budget";** negative variances and indices below 1 are bad. → [[planning-and-scheduling/35-earned-value|35]]

## About this course

- **Size:** 35 lessons and a question bank.
- **History:** written in November 2026 from the author's own study notes for SSG 513 (Techniques of Planning and Scheduling), which merge the lecture slides (including the SIMS, Makwana and MGMT 4135 sets), class exercises, assignments, and the 2019/20, 2020/21, 2023/24 and 2024/25 papers and marking schemes.
- **Corrections:** the 2020/21 PERT marking scheme gives the critical-path variance as 15.03, but its own five numbers add to 10.53; the question bank uses 10.53. The 2020/21 Brooks' table ends at 31.34 weeks; rerunning it with the question's figures gives 29.33 (lesson 31). The notes stop at the normal cost for the 2019/20 crash table; lesson 23 finishes the crashing (10 days, N63,333).
- **Readings to confirm:** "slack or free float" is the lecturer's name for LS − ES, which is strictly total float (lesson 14); the 2023/24 fixed cost is read as per day (lesson 24); the 2024/25 e-library columns are read by size (lesson 19).
- **Not in the slides:** the ten knowledge-area outlines, rolling wave planning, the two reserves, bar charts, line of balance, time-location charts and earned value are written from general project-management knowledge. The fence's worker numbers, its smoothing, the four-contract scoring example and the small Brooks example are added for teaching.
- **Diagrams** come from `scripts/diagrams/planning_scheduling.py`, which also checks the worked numbers in the lessons.
