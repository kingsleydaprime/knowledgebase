# The Project Environment and Key Terms

A project's environment is everything around it that can help it, hurt it or leave it alone, and it splits into what is inside the organisation and what is outside.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: 5ac1999c-484d-4961-8e03-9341cfb7bae6 -->

You should have read [[planning-and-scheduling/01-what-a-project-is|01 — What a Project Is]].

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. sort environment factors into internal and external, with examples;
2. tell product from outcome, and project from program and portfolio;
3. define project management and say what a project manager is for;
4. tell a tool from a technique.

## What the project environment is
<!-- bite: f370c9d4-d331-401e-a70f-98eceb88bc9a -->

Remember the fence and gate from lesson 1? It isn't built in empty space. The company building it has its own templates, its own workers and their skills, maybe some scheduling software. That's the **internal** environment. Outside the company there is the weather, the price of wood, estate rules and the cost of borrowing money. That's the **external** environment.

Any of these can help (a dry week, a skilled foreman), hurt (rain, a sudden price rise) or do nothing at all. A planner has to know which ones matter, because a plan that ignores them breaks the first time they change.

## Inside the organisation
<!-- bite: bdead1dc-3ec6-4ecd-8222-551ac513839d -->

The slides list these internal factors:

1. **Process assets**: the tools, templates and methods the organisation already has, such as a standard fence-building checklist.
2. **Governance documents**: the rules for how projects are approved and run.
3. **Knowledge assets**: lessons learned from earlier projects.
4. **Security and safety**: the policies workers must follow on site.
5. **Culture and structure**: how decisions get made and who reports to whom.
6. **Location of people and facilities**: whether the team and yard are near the site.
7. **Infrastructure**: buildings, vehicles, IT.
8. **Scheduling software**: MS Project, Primavera, or none.
9. **Resource availability**: how many workers and machines are free.
10. **Employee skills**: what the team can actually do.

## Outside the organisation
<!-- bite: 320b4379-e662-46bb-89c9-95d9f1b3842c -->

And these external ones:

1. **Market conditions**: who else is competing for the same workers and materials.
2. **Social and cultural issues**: what the community expects.
3. **Regulations**: building codes, permits, estate rules.
4. **Commercial databases**: published cost and productivity data.
5. **Academic research**: new methods and materials.
6. **Industry standards**: the agreed ways of doing the work.
7. **Financial factors**: exchange rates, interest, inflation and taxes.
8. **Physical environment**: weather and working conditions.

Easy way to sort one: ask "can the organisation change this by deciding to?" If yes, it is internal. Nobody in the company can decide the weather or the exchange rate, so those are external.

## Words that are easy to mix up
<!-- bite: 8c5b57ed-b6f6-4ab3-a4b8-24e1dd0b4ed7 -->

These come up in definitions questions, and they sound alike.

1. **Product**: This is something produced that you can count. It is either an end item (the finished fence) or a component (one gate).
2. **Outcome**: This is the end result and the benefit it brings, which is wider than an output. The fence is the output; a secure compound is the outcome.
3. **Project**: This is one unique, temporary effort, like the fence.
4. **Program**: This is a group of related projects managed together to get benefits you wouldn't get by running them one by one. An estate developer's "fence every plot" program shares one wood supplier and one crew.
5. **Portfolio**: This is a collection of projects, programs and operations managed as a group to meet strategic goals. The developer's whole business is its portfolio.

## Project management and the project manager
<!-- bite: b7cced00-ac8d-4b5b-9e93-1e376906b5b5 -->

1. **Project management**: This is applying knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to meet the project's requirements.
2. **Project manager**: This is the person the performing organisation assigns to lead the team that is responsible for achieving the project's objectives.

Inside the first definition are two words worth separating:

1. **Tool**: This is something you use, like a template or a piece of software. Examples from class: the work breakdown structure (WBS), the Gantt chart, MS Project and Primavera.
2. **Technique**: This is a way of doing something, a method you follow. Examples: decomposition, activity on node (AON) drawing, estimating, rolling wave planning, earned value and resource levelling.

Most of this subject is learning those tools and techniques. And the first thing they help you manage is a set of three limits every project lives under. That's the next lesson.

## Common pitfalls
<!-- bite: dd2f7ef8-a687-488e-b4eb-1d22c4d26d27 -->

1. **Calling resource availability external.** It is internal: it's the organisation's own workers and machines.
2. **Mixing output and outcome.** The fence is the output. Security is the outcome.
3. **Mixing program and portfolio.** A program is related projects for shared benefit. A portfolio is everything grouped for strategy, operations included.

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: 5a1f8889-c378-4ff6-955b-8e47285a76ec -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: mcq id="1fa8c6e2" -->
   Which of these are part of a project's **external** environment? Choose all that apply.

   - [x] A rise in interest rates
   - [ ] The skills of the company's own engineers
   - [x] A new government building permit rule
   - [ ] The company's project templates
   - [x] Heavy rain during the build

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Interest rates, regulations and weather are outside the organisation's control. Staff skills and templates are internal.

   </details>

2. <!-- q: mcq id="7d2b5e09" -->
   A telecom company runs ten tower-building projects together so they can share one crane and one steel supplier. What is this group called?

   - [ ] A portfolio
   - [x] A program
   - [ ] An operation
   - [ ] A work package

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   A program: related projects managed together for a benefit (shared crane and supplier) they wouldn't get one by one.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: gap id="b8e4013c" answers="output|product;outcome" -->
   A new borehole is the ___; clean water for the village every day is the ___.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Output (or product), outcome. The outcome is the benefit the output brings.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: mcq id="c35a9f71" -->
   Which of these is a technique rather than a tool?

   - [ ] MS Project
   - [ ] A Gantt chart template
   - [x] Rolling wave planning
   - [ ] Primavera

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Rolling wave planning is a method you follow. The others are things you use.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: open id="2e96d4b7" -->
   Give two internal and two external environment factors that could delay a school-building project, and say why each one is internal or external.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Internal (the organisation controls them): too few masons free (resource availability), no scheduling software so clashes go unseen. External (it can't decide them): rainy season flooding the site (physical environment), a cement price jump (market or financial factors).

   </details>
