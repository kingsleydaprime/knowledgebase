# What a Project Is

A project is a temporary effort to create something unique, and it ends when that thing is handed over.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: baa69e33-82cd-4ee4-9c4e-b9d14d8fc7ea -->

No background is needed. This is the first lesson of the subject.

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. define a project in your own words and in the PMBOK words;
2. tell a project from an operation;
3. list the nine characteristics of a project and explain each with an example.

## What a project is
<!-- bite: 0ddcb780-cb82-4102-bc4a-4aff668f3adc -->

Think of building a fence and a gate around a compound. You dig the holes, buy the wood and the gate, put them up and paint them. When the last coat of paint is dry, the job is done and the workers go home. That is a project: it has a start, a finish, and it produces one particular thing.

Now think of the bakery down the road that bakes bread every morning. Nobody hands over "the bread" and closes the bakery. The work goes on and repeats. That is an **operation**, not a project.

We'll keep coming back to the fence and gate. By the end of the subject you'll have broken it into activities, drawn it as a network, found how long it takes and which jobs can't slip, and fitted it to the workers you really have.

## How the standards bodies say it
<!-- bite: e9a5cba4-2cd9-48d3-8b50-6f843e865573 -->

PMBOK, the guide written by the Project Management Institute (PMI), defines a project in its 7th edition like this:

A one-sentence version for an exam: a project is a **temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result**.

## Five bodies, one idea
<!-- bite: 7fbe8d1d-b513-48e1-be58-c288d6ddce12 -->

The other standards bodies define a project in their own words, and they agree on the core.

Other bodies say the same thing from their own angle:

1. **PMBOK (PMI)**: temporary, and it creates a unique product, service or result.
2. **IPMA**: it is limited by time and cost, and it delivers a set of defined deliverables to quality standards.
3. **ISO**: it is a unique set of coordinated, controlled activities with start and end dates.
4. **APM**: it is a unique, transient endeavour to achieve planned objectives, which may be outputs, outcomes or benefits.
5. **PRINCE2**: it is a temporary organisation created to deliver business products to an agreed business case.

Notice what they all share: **temporary** and **unique**. Those two words are what separate a project from an operation.

## Project or operation?
<!-- bite: 52cad8d3-3a9d-4f40-825e-bc36cd440665 -->

An operation carries on and repeats. A project ends when its deliverables are handed over.

1. Building the fence is a project. Opening the gate every morning for the next ten years is an operation.
2. Writing a new banking app is a project. Running the bank's servers every day after launch is an operation.
3. Organising one wedding is a project. A caterer cooking for weddings every weekend is running an operation, even though each wedding is a project for the couple.

The test: ask "when does this end, and what gets handed over?" If there is no answer, it is probably an operation.

## The nine characteristics of a project
<!-- bite: 91bff1e6-00c5-4c63-a752-13563ef63891 -->

Exams ask for any five of these (2019/20, 2023/24 and 2024/25 papers). Learn all nine so you can pick the five you explain best. The fence and gate carries the examples.

1. **Objectives**: a project has a mission to achieve. The fence's objective is a closed, painted compound.
2. **Lifecycle**: it moves through phases from start to close: plan the fence, build it, hand it over.
3. **Definite time frame**: it has a start date and a finish date.
4. **Uniqueness**: no two projects are the same. The next fence has a different ground, length and gate.
5. **Teamwork**: success needs people to coordinate. The painter can't start until the builders finish.

## The nine characteristics: the other four
<!-- bite: 8630f034-be0b-4d39-822e-ce27d967145d -->

6. **Complexity**: it is made of many linked activities. Even the fence has eight, and some must wait for others.
7. **Risk and uncertainty**: the future is not fully known. Rain may stop the digging, or the wood may arrive late.
8. **Change**: scope, plans and conditions shift as it runs. The owner may ask for a second gate halfway through.
9. **Response to environment**: it reacts to its surroundings, such as a new estate rule on fence height.

When the exam says "explain", one line of meaning plus one example like these earns the mark. A bare list of nine words does not.

The characteristics already hint at the rest of the subject. Complexity is why we break work down and draw networks. Risk and uncertainty is why PERT exists. Change is why plans are revised. But before any of that, a project sits inside an organisation and a wider world, and the next lesson looks at what surrounds it.

## Common pitfalls
<!-- bite: 10f95a17-68b8-4265-b237-e83ea4592da8 -->

1. **Calling repeated work a project.** If it never ends and nothing is handed over, it is an operation.
2. **Thinking "temporary" means "short".** A refinery build can run for years and still be temporary, because it has an end.
3. **Listing characteristics without explaining them.** "Uniqueness" alone gets little; "no two projects are the same, the next fence has different ground" gets the mark.

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: a240d1ed-c112-46d8-86ea-e0a45aef3655 -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: mcq id="5b1e7c20" -->
   Which of these is a project rather than an operation?

   - [ ] A hospital pharmacy dispensing drugs every day
   - [x] Installing a new X-ray machine in the hospital
   - [ ] A security guard locking the gates every night
   - [ ] Paying staff salaries every month

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Installing the X-ray machine. It has a start and a finish and delivers one unique result. The other three repeat with no end.

   </details>

2. <!-- q: gap id="9c03a4d1" answers="temporary;unique" -->
   PMBOK defines a project as a ___ endeavour undertaken to create a ___ product, service or result.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Temporary, unique. Those two words separate a project from an operation.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="e2f7a915" -->
   A road contractor finds halfway through that the client now wants street lights added. Which characteristic of a project does this show?

   - [ ] Uniqueness
   - [ ] Definite time frame
   - [x] Change
   - [ ] Teamwork

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Change: scope and plans shift while the project runs.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: open id="40ad8b6e" -->
   A five-year dam build is called "temporary". Explain why that word still fits.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Temporary means the effort has a definite end, not that it is short. When the dam is handed over, the project ends and the team breaks up, even after five years. Running the dam afterwards is an operation.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: open id="d7158c3a" -->
   Explain any three characteristics of a project, with an example of each from a wedding you are organising.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Any three, each with meaning and example. For instance: objectives (a mission to achieve: a happy, well-run wedding day); definite time frame (it starts when planning begins and ends on the wedding day); teamwork (the caterer, decorator and DJ must coordinate, since the DJ can't set up until the hall is decorated).

   </details>
