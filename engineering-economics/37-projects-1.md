# Projects 1: Projects, the Project Cycle and Planning

A project is a one-off, temporary effort with a clear start and end, a budget and a specific objective.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: b0a6b531-cefe-445b-b034-3c5c9c0a853e -->

You should know capital budgeting from [[engineering-economics/35-capital-budgeting-1|Capital Budgeting 1]] and [[engineering-economics/36-capital-budgeting-2|Capital Budgeting 2]].

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. define a project, a programme, the project cycle, economic analysis, project evaluation and the other basic terms;

3. describe the levels of plan, and explain vision, mission and succession planning.

## What a project is
<!-- bite: 59d22a9c-1176-490e-843f-9c28b95f20c7 -->

> **Flag:** your lecture materials don't cover this topic, but your 2017/2018 paper has 50 multiple-choice questions and a written section on it. Lessons 37 and 38 are written from general knowledge, so check them against your lecturer's wording.

Building a new hostel is a project: it starts, it finishes, it has a budget and one clear goal. Running the hostel afterwards is not a project, it's ongoing operations. That difference, temporary versus ongoing, is the heart of the definition.

It links to capital budgeting (lessons 35 and 36), which decides *which* projects to fund, and to lesson 01, which says what engineering economics is for.

## Basic definitions 1
<!-- bite: 8ea626b7-3df8-42a5-8a0e-6505ef036e28 -->

1. **Project:** This is a one-off, temporary effort with a clear start and end, a budget and a specific objective. Projects differ in complexity, duration and scope.
2. **Programme:** This is a group of related projects managed together to achieve a bigger objective than any single project could. A state's "schools for all" programme might contain fifty school-building projects.
3. **Project cycle:** This is the stages a project passes through from idea to completion: idea, identification, evaluation (appraisal), selection, execution (implementation), then operation and review.
4. **Economic analysis:** This is studying whether a project is good for the economy or society, using social costs and benefits (shadow prices), not just the firm's profit.

## Basic definitions 2
<!-- bite: 730d0d1c-9b73-48ba-9c39-5f228a98a5d9 -->

5. **Project evaluation:** This is judging the worth of a project, before it starts (is it worth doing?) and after (did it deliver?), using criteria such as NPV, IRR and BCR from part 3.
6. **Financial analysis:** This is checking if the project is viable and profitable for the investors in money terms.
7. **Portfolio analysis:** This is assessing the attractiveness of the firm's current businesses or products and the market, to guide future investment.
8. **Technical and economic feasibility study:** This is the detailed investigation of a project, covering the market, technical, financial, economic and ecological sides (lesson 36).

## The three objectives of a project
<!-- bite: 7c8273ac-ee2d-4152-913c-69d0b6f8b843 -->

**Three main objectives of a project:** quality (performance), time and cost (budget). A good project manager balances the three.

They pull against each other. Want it faster? It'll cost more, or quality will drop. Want it cheaper? It'll take longer, or quality will drop. You rarely get all three, so the manager decides which matters most for this project.

## Planning
<!-- bite: d4500afe-282d-45af-a7ac-9284a34b4344 -->

Planning means deciding what to do and how. It includes the scope statement, the project plan and the schedule. It reduces risk and increases efficiency.

**Levels of plan:**

1. **Visionary plan:** the long-term picture.
2. **Strategic plan:** top management, long term.
3. **Tactical plan:** short range, breaks the mission into smaller actionable pieces.
4. **Operational plan:** day to day.

## Vision, mission and succession
<!-- bite: e846e52d-8422-4166-8ef2-da03dde3802c -->

- **Vision statement:** where the organisation wants to be in the future (for example in five years).
- **Mission statement:** a realistic overview of what the organisation does, its aims and ambitions.
- **Succession planning:** identifying and developing internal people to fill key leadership positions.

A quick way to tell vision from mission: vision is *where we're going*, mission is *what we do every day to get there*.

Planning says what to do. The next lesson covers the tools that schedule and control it, and the people and documents that make it happen.
