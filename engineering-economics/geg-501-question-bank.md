### Question 1(b)

A manufacturing firm is considering installing an automated production line costing ₦85 million. The system is expected to generate annual net cash inflows of ₦18 million for 8 years. The salvage value at the end of year 8 is estimated at ₦10 million. The firm's MARR is 14%.

- **(a)** Determine the present worth (PW) of the investment. *(5 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/11-comparing-alternatives-1|11 — Comparing Alternatives 1]], with the P/A and P/F factors in [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06 — Interest Factors 1]]
- **(b)** Compute the equivalent annual worth (EAW). *(5 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/12-comparing-alternatives-2|12 — Comparing Alternatives 2]]
- **(c)** Determine the internal rate of return (IRR). *(5 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/13-comparing-alternatives-3|13 — Comparing Alternatives 3]] (try rates until PW = 0, then interpolate)
- **(d)** Based on your results, advise whether the investment should be accepted. *(2 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/14-comparing-alternatives-4|14 — Comparing Alternatives 4]] (the quick guide)
- **(e)** Briefly explain why IRR and PW may sometimes give conflicting rankings for mutually exclusive projects. *(3 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/13-comparing-alternatives-3|13 — Comparing Alternatives 3]]

### Question 2

A company purchases industrial equipment for ₦120 million. The equipment has a 6-year useful life and zero salvage value. The corporate tax rate is 30%. Inflation is 9% annually and the real MARR is 10%.

- **(a)(i)** Annual depreciation using straight line (SL). *(4 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/16-depreciation-2|16 — Depreciation 2]]
- **(a)(ii)** Annual depreciation using double declining balance (DDB). *(4 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/16-depreciation-2|16 — Depreciation 2]] (use k = 2/n)
- **(b)** Annual tax savings from depreciation under both methods. *(4 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/18-depreciation-4|18 — Depreciation 4]]
- **(c)** Convert the real MARR to nominal MARR using the Fisher equation. *(3 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/09-inflation-1|09 — Inflation 1]]
- **(d)** Explain how the depreciation method affects (i) cash flow timing, (ii) net present value, (iii) tax liability. *(3 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/18-depreciation-4|18 — Depreciation 4]]
- **(e)** Under inflationary conditions, discuss why real vs nominal cash flow consistency is critical in project evaluation. *(2 marks)* → [[engineering-economics/09-inflation-1|09 — Inflation 1]] and [[engineering-economics/18-depreciation-4|18 — Depreciation 4]]

### Question 4

BA produces and sells two products. W sells for ₦8 per unit with a total variable cost of ₦3.80. R sells for ₦14 per unit with a total variable cost of ₦4.20. For every five units of W sold, six units of R are sold. BA's fixed costs are ₦43,000 per period.

- **(i)** Calculate the margin of safety in terms of sales revenue and also as a percentage of budgeted sales revenue. *(4 marks)*
- **(ii)** Calculate the contribution per mix. *(4 marks)*
- **(iii)** Calculate the break-even point in terms of the number of mixes. *(4 marks)*
- **(iv)** Calculate the break-even in terms of the number of units of products. *(4 marks)*
- **(v)** Calculate the margin of safety. *(4 marks)*

→ [[engineering-economics/28-break-even-analysis-3|28 — Break-Even Analysis 3]]

> The scan does not show the budgeted sales figure for part (i), so your paper may have given it separately. If it did not, ask your lecturer which figure to use. Note too that the fixed cost here (₦43,000) differs from the lecture's similar example (₦43,890), so the number of mixes won't come out whole.

### The booklet's time value of money questions

Amounts are in rupees in the booklet. Indian digit grouping writes 10,00,000 for 1,000,000. The booklet has no question 6 in this set, so the seventh question is numbered 7.

1. A company wants to set up a reserve that provides an equivalent annual amount of Rs. 10,00,000 for the next 20 years. The reserve grows at 15% annually. Find the single payment to make now. → [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06 — Interest Factors 1]] (P/A)
2. A bank lends a company Rs. 10,00,000 at 18% compounded annually, to be repaid in 15 equal yearly instalments. Find the instalment. → [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06 — Interest Factors 1]] (A/P)
3. A person wants a future sum of Rs. 1,00,000 after 10 years. What single deposit now, at 15% compounded annually? → [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06 — Interest Factors 1]] (P/F)
4. A company must replace a facility after 15 years at a cost of Rs. 5,00,000. It deposits an equal amount at the end of each year at 18% compounded annually. Find the yearly deposit. → [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06 — Interest Factors 1]] (A/F, sinking fund)
5. An investment project has a net cash flow of −Rs. 6,50,000 in year 0 and Rs. 1,62,500 each year from year 1 to year 8. The firm's interest rate is 15%. Compute the NPW and say if the project is acceptable. → [[engineering-economics/11-comparing-alternatives-1|11 — Comparing Alternatives 1]]
6. A machine costs Rs. 40,000 and has a 10-year useful life. At the end of year 10 it can be sold for Rs. 5,000 after tax adjustment. The firm could earn an after-tax revenue of Rs. 10,000 a year with the machine. Should it be bought at an interest rate of 15% compounded annually? → [[engineering-economics/11-comparing-alternatives-1|11]] and [[engineering-economics/12-comparing-alternatives-2|12 — Comparing Alternatives 2]]
7. A bridge costs Rs. 40,00,000, lasts 15 years, and costs Rs. 1,50,000 a year to operate and maintain. Fuel savings are Rs. 6,00,000 in the first year and rise by Rs. 50,000 every year after. Using a 12% interest rate, check whether the project is justified on the benefit-cost ratio. → [[engineering-economics/14-comparing-alternatives-4|14 — Comparing Alternatives 4]] and the gradient series in [[engineering-economics/07-interest-factors-2|07 — Interest Factors 2]]
