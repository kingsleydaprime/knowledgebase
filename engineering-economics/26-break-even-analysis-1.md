# Break-Even Analysis 1: Contribution and the Break-Even Point

Break-even analysis, part of cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis, finds how much a firm must sell before it stops losing money.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: e01b5720-09d9-4c26-a76c-5f053cc5e2c2 -->

You should know fixed and variable cost and the cost equation from [[engineering-economics/02-cost-concepts-1|Cost Concepts 1]].

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. state the assumptions of CVP analysis;
2. calculate contribution per unit, the C/S ratio, the break-even point in units and in sales value, and the sales needed for a target profit;
3. calculate the margin of safety, and read a break-even chart.

## What break-even analysis is
<!-- bite: 2cd24baf-3374-4ae4-9014-d8c960499a65 -->

This uses the fixed and variable costs from lesson 02 to answer a simple question: how much must we sell before we stop losing money? It also shows how risky a project is, which links to part 6.

Think of a small pure water business. Every month you pay rent and the machine loan whether you sell one bag or a thousand: that's fixed cost. Every bag also costs you water, nylon and power: that's variable cost. Each bag you sell for more than its variable cost chips away at the fixed cost. Sell enough bags and the fixed cost is covered. That number of bags is your break-even point.

## What CVP analysis answers
<!-- bite: 98bbd21a-9267-4d44-a0b3-a88703f9a077 -->

CVP analysis looks at how sales volume, selling price, cost structure and profit affect one another. It helps answer:

- how many units to sell to break even;
- how many to earn a target profit;
- whether to invest in automation;
- whether more advertising pays.

## Assumptions
<!-- bite: 7cdecda1-3b7f-4631-9092-ad98d2776018 -->

- Selling price is constant.
- Costs are linear and can be split into variable and fixed parts.
- In a multi-product firm, the sales mix is constant.
- Inventories do not change (what is produced is sold).

The 2025/2026 exam asked for five assumptions. A fifth that follows from the second: fixed costs stay the same in total, and variable cost per unit stays the same, over the range of output being studied.

## Contribution and the C/S ratio
<!-- bite: c9aed9d4-de87-4ec8-bcde-37fce031027b -->

- **Profit = Sales − variable expenses − fixed expenses.**
- **Contribution per unit (unit CM)** = selling price − variable cost per unit. It is what each unit contributes towards fixed cost and profit.
- **Contribution to sales ratio (C/S ratio, also called the P/V or profit-volume ratio, or CM ratio)** = contribution ÷ sales × 100%. It equals 1 − the variable cost ratio.

In the pure water example: if a bag sells for ₦50 and costs ₦30 to make, each bag contributes ₦20 to fixed cost and profit. The C/S ratio is 20/50 = 40%: 40 kobo of every naira of sales is contribution.

## The break-even point (BEP)
<!-- bite: 0c17a430-7162-4cf9-8e01-f55835d15891 -->

The **break-even point** is the activity level where there is neither profit nor loss. Sales equal total cost, or total contribution equals fixed cost.

So you ask how many contributions it takes to cover the fixed cost. In units, divide by the contribution per unit. In naira of sales, divide by the C/S ratio:

$$\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed costs}}{\text{Contribution per unit}} \qquad \text{BEP (sales value)} = \frac{\text{Fixed costs}}{\text{C/S ratio}}$$

Pure water again: fixed cost ₦40,000 a month. BEP = 40,000 ÷ 20 = 2,000 bags, or 40,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₦100,000 of sales.

## Target profit
<!-- bite: 1c9a5254-b529-423f-8bab-2ddd41a9dd5c -->

Break-even is the special case where the target profit is zero. For any target profit, the contributions must cover the fixed cost *and* the profit:

$$\text{Units for target profit} = \frac{\text{Fixed costs} + \text{Target profit}}{\text{Contribution per unit}} \qquad \text{Sales value} = \frac{\text{Fixed costs} + \text{Target profit}}{\text{C/S ratio}}$$

To make ₦10,000 profit a month: (40,000 + 10,000) ÷ 20 = 2,500 bags.

## Margin of safety
<!-- bite: a9740af9-c562-442b-ab0b-22cc2a2c2592 -->

The **margin of safety** is how far sales can fall before you make a loss. It's the gap between what you plan to sell and break-even, in naira or as a percentage of the budget:

$$\text{Margin of safety} = \text{Budgeted sales} - \text{Break-even sales} \qquad \text{MOS \%} = \frac{\text{Budgeted sales} - \text{Break-even sales}}{\text{Budgeted sales}} \times 100$$

If the pure water business budgets 3,000 bags, the margin of safety is 1,000 bags, or 33.3%. Sales can fall by a third before it loses money.

Two methods give the same answers: the **equation method** (sales = variable cost + fixed cost + profit) and the **contribution method** (the formulas above).

## Break-even chart (graphical method)
<!-- bite: 65ad67fe-b777-419a-8730-0d2dd6a0929f -->

Plot sales value or units on the horizontal axis and money on the vertical axis. Draw the fixed cost line (flat), then add variable cost to get the total cost line, then draw the total revenue line from the origin. They cross at the break-even point.

![Break-even chart for the lecture 9 exercise: units from 0 to 12,000 on the horizontal axis and naira on the vertical axis, a flat fixed cost line at 340,000, a total cost line rising from 340,000 at 89 naira per unit, and a revenue line rising from zero at 129 naira per unit, crossing total cost at the break-even point of 8,500 units; budgeted sales of 10,000 units are marked, with the gap from 8,500 to 10,000 shaded as the margin of safety](images/ee-break-even-chart.svg)

The gap between revenue and total cost is profit (or loss), and the gap from the break-even point to the budgeted sales level is the margin of safety. The picture uses the lecture 9 exercise, which lesson 27 works through.

The **P/V chart** plots profit against sales instead, starting at a loss equal to the fixed cost, and crosses zero at break-even.

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: f938176e-7f15-47cc-ac26-c07d7f929e3c -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: exact id="a2ac4af9" answer="2500" accept="2,500" -->
   A firm sells a product for ₦80 with a variable cost of ₦50 per unit. Its fixed costs are ₦75,000. What is its break-even point in units?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Contribution per unit = 80 − 50 = ₦30. BEP = 75,000 ÷ 30 = 2,500 units.

   </details>

2. <!-- q: exact id="6e38e15c" answer="0.375" accept="37.5%|37.5" -->
   A product sells for ₦80 with a variable cost of ₦50 per unit. What is its C/S ratio, as a decimal?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   C/S = contribution ÷ sales = 30 ÷ 80 = 0.375, or 37.5%.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: exact id="1b132570" answer="200000" accept="200,000|₦200,000" -->
   A firm's C/S ratio is 37.5% and its fixed costs are ₦75,000. What is its break-even point in sales value, in naira?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   BEP (sales) = 75,000 ÷ 0.375 = ₦200,000. (Check: 2,500 units × ₦80 = ₦200,000.)

   </details>

4. <!-- q: exact id="ef1f6727" answer="3500" accept="3,500" -->
   A product sells for ₦80 with a variable cost of ₦50 per unit, and fixed costs are ₦75,000. How many units must be sold for a profit of ₦30,000?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   (75,000 + 30,000) ÷ 30 = 105,000 ÷ 30 = 3,500 units.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: exact id="5308a1b9" answer="20" accept="20%" -->
   A firm budgets sales of ₦250,000 and its break-even sales are ₦200,000. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   MOS = 250,000 − 200,000 = ₦50,000. MOS % = 50,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 20%.

   </details>

6. <!-- q: open id="78f8cef8" -->
   Identify and briefly discuss five assumptions underlying cost-volume-profit analysis. (A 5-mark part of the 2025/2026 exam.)

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   1. Selling price is constant: every unit sells at the same price, whatever the volume.
   2. Costs are linear and can be split into fixed and variable parts.
   3. Fixed costs stay the same in total and variable cost per unit stays the same, within the relevant range of output.
   4. In a multi-product firm, the sales mix is constant.
   5. Inventories do not change: what is produced is sold.

   </details>
