# Depreciation 1: What It Is and Why We Depreciate

Depreciation is the reduction in the economic value of an asset over time because of use, wear and tear, obsolescence or the passage of time.

After this lesson you will be able to:

## What depreciation is
<!-- bite: 22232174-e380-432c-a4a3-8630fb6aeb5e -->

In part 3 you compared projects using cash flows. Depreciation is different: it is not a cash flow, but it changes tax, profit and replacement decisions, so it still affects your cash analysis.

**Definition:** depreciation is the reduction in the economic value of an asset over time because of use, wear and tear, obsolescence or the passage of time. It is an **accounting allocation, not a cash outflow**.

Instead of charging the whole price of an asset in the year you buy it, you spread the cost over its life. Land is never depreciated.

From an economics view, depreciation reflects loss of service potential, not only physical decay.

## Why spread the cost?
<!-- bite: 0293987b-9d33-4370-95f1-ce14fad2bab6 -->

Picture a bus company that buys a ₦50 million bus that will run for 10 years. If it charged all ₦50 million to year 1, year 1 would show a huge loss and years 2 to 10 would look wonderfully profitable. Neither is true. The bus earns money every year, so its cost should be shared out over every year it works.

That sharing out is depreciation. No money leaves the company each year (the money left when the bus was bought). It's a bookkeeping entry. But because tax is charged on profit, that entry changes how much tax the company pays, and *that* is real cash. Lesson 18 follows that through.

## Causes of depreciation
<!-- bite: 07d8b05b-a333-4f1e-bafc-d2004ac1627e -->

| Cause | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Physical deterioration | Use, wear and tear, corrosion, exposure to weather |
| Functional obsolescence | The asset no longer meets the need: demand changed, a more efficient unit exists, or the work exceeds its capacity |
| Technological obsolescence | New technology makes the old asset uneconomical |
| Economic obsolescence | Changes in demand, regulation or market conditions |
| Time-related | A lease, patent or copyright expires, and assets age even without use |
| Accident | Sudden failure or damage |
| Depletion | Using up natural resources: oil, timber, minerals |
| Monetary | Price rises make replacement more expensive than the money recovered |
| Deferred maintenance | Value falls quickly when maintenance is delayed |

## Terms you must know
<!-- bite: c898ddd8-ae54-4061-b81b-c2a1a42f0518 -->

1. **First cost (FC or P):** This is the purchase and installation cost (price plus taxes and transport).
2. **Salvage value (SV or S):** This is the expected value at the end of useful life.
3. **Book value (BV):** This is the value recorded in the accounts at a given time. It equals the first cost minus the depreciation charged so far.
4. **Depreciable amount:** This is FC − SV, the total that gets written off over the life.
5. **Useful life (n):** This is the expected service life in years or units.
6. **Depreciation charge (D):** This is the amount allocated each year.

Property is depreciable if it is used in business to produce income, it wears out or loses value, and it has a life longer than one year.

## Why we depreciate
<!-- bite: 388efedd-fe8e-4265-8f27-951fd3ef63cb -->

1. Cost-based decisions.
2. Working out taxable income. Depreciation is deductible, so it reduces tax.
3. Estimating true project profit.
4. Replacement analysis.
5. Asset valuation and reporting.

## Replacement analysis: defender and challenger
<!-- bite: 36cc0f13-3ecb-425b-ac29-3dcab1355061 -->

Reason 4 deserves a closer look. The **defender** is the asset you already own and the **challenger** is the new one you could buy. Compare them using relevant future cash flows (not sunk costs) and pick the one with the better net present value.

So what you paid for the old machine years ago doesn't matter. Sunk cost (lesson 02) again. What matters is what each machine will cost and earn from today.

There are several ways to decide how much to write off each year. Every method writes off the same total, FC − SV, but at a different pace. The next lesson starts with the two most common ones.

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: 83e3710b-7874-4b44-b0f9-e333d6dd7bf8 -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: mcq id="2bc31110" -->
   Why is depreciation described as an accounting allocation, not a cash outflow?

   - [ ] Because it is paid to the government as tax
   - [x] Because no money leaves the firm when it is charged; it spreads a cost already paid over the asset's life
   - [ ] Because it only applies to land
   - [ ] Because it is the same as salvage value

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   No money leaves the firm when depreciation is charged. The cash left when the asset was bought; depreciation spreads that cost over the years the asset is used.

   </details>

2. <!-- q: mcq id="75bea45b" -->
   A firm's five-year-old printers still work, but new printers are twice as fast and use half the power, so the old ones are no longer economical. What cause of depreciation is this?

   - [ ] Physical deterioration
   - [ ] Depletion
   - [x] Technological obsolescence
   - [ ] Accident

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Technological obsolescence: new technology makes the old asset uneconomical, even though it still works.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: exact id="f4cae848" answer="4500000" accept="4,500,000|₦4,500,000" -->
   A truck has a first cost of ₦5,000,000 and a salvage value of ₦500,000. What is its depreciable amount, in naira?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Depreciable amount = FC − SV = 5,000,000 − 500,000 = ₦4,500,000.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: exact id="e4740876" answer="3800000" accept="3,800,000|₦3,800,000" -->
   A machine cost ₦6,000,000. So far ₦2,200,000 of depreciation has been charged on it. What is its book value, in naira?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Book value = first cost − depreciation charged so far = 6,000,000 − 2,200,000 = ₦3,800,000.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: mcq id="0646e876" -->
   Which of these is never depreciated?

   - [ ] A factory building
   - [x] Land
   - [ ] A delivery van
   - [ ] A computer

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Land. It does not wear out or get used up, so it is never depreciated.

   </details>

6. <!-- q: open id="2ef1eec5" -->
   In replacement analysis, what are the defender and the challenger, and how do you choose between them?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The defender is the asset you already own, and the challenger is the new asset you could buy to replace it. You compare them using relevant future cash flows only, ignoring sunk costs such as what was paid for the defender, and pick the one with the better net present value.

   </details>
