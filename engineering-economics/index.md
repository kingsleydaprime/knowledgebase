# Engineering Economics

## What engineering economics is

Engineering economics is the use of economic ideas and tools to judge engineering options and help you decide between them. An engineering solution is only good if it also makes financial sense.

You've already done it in small ways. Choosing between fixing an old generator and buying a new one, deciding whether solar panels pay for themselves, or working out whether to pay for something now or in instalments: each of those is an engineering economics question. This subject gives you the tools to answer them with numbers.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end you will be able to:
- sort costs into types and build up a total cost;
- move money forward and back in time, so sums paid in different years can be compared fairly;
- allow for interest compounded more than once a year, and for inflation and its effect on tax;
- decide whether a project is worth doing, and choose between projects, with six different methods;
- work out depreciation four ways, and its effect on tax and cash flow;
- estimate what a project will cost, and test how risky the decision is;
- find how much a firm must sell before it stops losing money;
- explain demand, supply, production and banking, the economics behind costs and prices;
- describe how firms plan, choose and manage big projects.

## Key ideas to remember

Once you've read the lessons, these are the points worth keeping. Each links to where it's explained.

1. **Ignore sunk costs.** Money already spent is gone whichever option you choose, so compare only what happens from now on. → [[engineering-economics/02-cost-concepts-1|02]]
2. **Never add money from different years.** Bring every cash flow to one point in time first, at the same interest rate. → [[engineering-economics/05-time-value-of-money-2|05]]
3. **A factor reads "find the first, given the second".** P/F gives a present value from a future one. → [[engineering-economics/06-interest-factors-1|06]]
4. **Real and nominal never mix.** Discount real cash flows with a real rate and nominal ones with a nominal rate. → [[engineering-economics/09-inflation-1|09]]
5. **Present worth for equal lives, annual worth for different lives.** A correct analysis gives the same decision either way. → [[engineering-economics/12-comparing-alternatives-2|12]]
6. **Don't rank by IRR or BCR alone.** Check the extra investment against the MARR. → [[engineering-economics/13-comparing-alternatives-3|13]]
7. **Every depreciation method writes off FC − SV; only the pace changes.** Faster write-off brings the tax saving sooner, which is worth more. → [[engineering-economics/18-depreciation-4|18]]
8. **Break-even units = fixed cost ÷ contribution per unit.** → [[engineering-economics/26-break-even-analysis-1|26]]
