# Banking

A bank is an institution that deals in money and credit: it accepts deposits from the public, lends the money to those who need it, and helps move money from place to place.

## What a bank is
<!-- bite: a723e60c-8f26-4537-8d45-0f95ff17bb80 -->

Every project in this course needs money, and much of it is borrowed. The interest rate a firm pays for that money feeds straight into its cost of capital and its MARR. So it's worth knowing who lends it and how.

A bank is an institution that deals in **money and credit**. It accepts deposits from the public, lends the money to those who need it, and helps move money from place to place. Banks aim for profit, create credit, and create demand deposits that work as money, so they run the country's payment system.

## Functions of commercial banks 1: deposits
<!-- bite: a2b0dc94-bb14-4175-b06a-4eda484ed07d -->

**Accepting deposits:** from people who can save but cannot use the savings profitably themselves. The types:

1. **Fixed:** higher interest, locked for a period.
2. **Current:** for traders, unlimited withdrawals, usually no interest.
3. **Savings:** encourages small savings, with limits on withdrawals.
4. **Recurring:** regular monthly instalments.
5. **Home safe accounts.**

## Functions of commercial banks 2: loans
<!-- bite: c415424c-d925-4f99-86ab-4fbc2abeb40d -->

**Advancing loans:** after keeping cash reserves, banks lend the rest. The forms:

1. **Money at call:** very short, between banks.
2. **Cash credit:** against stocks and shares.
3. **Overdraft:** withdrawing more than your balance and paying interest on it.
4. **Discounting bills of exchange:** self-liquidating.
5. **Term loans:** more than one year.

## Functions of commercial banks 3: credit creation
<!-- bite: 332e91eb-1fc0-4746-af9e-b26e916f27f2 -->

**Credit creation:** when a bank grants a loan it opens an account in the borrower's name and credits it, so it creates a deposit of the same size. Deposits therefore grow by a multiple of reserves.

Here's how. You deposit ₦100,000. The bank keeps some as reserve and lends the rest to a contractor, crediting the contractor's account. The contractor pays a supplier, who deposits it in a bank, which lends most of it again. Each round creates new deposits, so the total grows to several times your original ₦100,000.

## Functions of commercial banks 4: the rest
<!-- bite: ca001dc7-ca87-4a07-a366-80f056474052 -->

- **Cheque system:** a cheque is the most developed credit instrument for payments.
- **Agency functions:** remitting funds, collecting and paying cheques and bills, carrying out standing orders, buying and selling securities, collecting dividends, tax advice, acting as trustee or executor, and acting as a representative for customers.
- **General utility functions:** lockers, traveller's cheques, letters of credit (useful in foreign trade), collecting statistics, underwriting securities, giving references on customers, and foreign exchange business.

## Role of commercial banks in a developing economy
<!-- bite: 42ee9fa5-0020-471c-86fc-10043ef675a1 -->

A good banking system is a pre-condition for development. Banks do more than deal in money: they lead development.

1. **Capital formation:** the key driver of growth, in three stages: generating savings, mobilising savings and channelling them into productive use. Banks help at all three (interest rewards savers, branches collect savings, loans send money to investors).
2. **Encouraging innovation:** entrepreneurs often lack funds, and bank loans let them start new ventures and expand.
3. **Monetisation of the economy:** banks create and distribute money, moving people from barter to money transactions, and extend into rural areas.
4. **Influencing economic activity** through interest rates and the availability of credit. Lower rates make investment more profitable and stimulate activity, higher rates discourage it.

Point 4 is engineering economics directly: a lower interest rate lowers the MARR, so more projects have a positive PW.

## The central bank
<!-- bite: d9da6ac7-fd1b-41d1-9106-114a1a9f3366 -->

> **Flag:** your handout's central bank section was cut off, and what remains describes the Reserve Bank of India. This part is general knowledge about the Central Bank of Nigeria, so check it against any extra notes you have.

The **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)** sits at the top of the banking system. Typical central bank functions:

1. **Issuer of currency:** it is the sole issuer of the naira.
2. **Banker and adviser to the government:** it keeps government accounts, makes and collects payments, and manages public debt.
3. **Banker to banks and lender of last resort:** banks keep reserves with it, and it lends to them in a crisis.
4. **Regulator and supervisor** of banks and other financial institutions.

## The central bank: policy and reserves
<!-- bite: b1d434e8-513f-452d-acc6-fd33dd51e621 -->

5. **Monetary policy and credit control:** it manages money supply and interest rates using tools such as the monetary policy rate, cash reserve requirement, liquidity ratio and open market operations.
6. **Custodian of foreign reserves** and manager of exchange rate policy.
7. **Developmental role:** promoting financial stability and the growth of financial institutions.

A central bank should not compete with commercial banks and should keep its assets liquid.

## The money market
<!-- bite: 9738014b-007b-4d7f-bc17-06e7b5999ae9 -->

> **Flag:** general knowledge; your handout's section was cut off.

The money market is where **short-term funds** (usually up to one year) are borrowed and lent. It gives governments, banks and companies a way to manage short-term cash needs.

Common instruments: treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, bankers' acceptances, call money and repurchase agreements.

Its rates guide the cost of short-term credit, which feeds into the interest rate (MARR and cost of capital) you used in parts 2 and 3.

   A trader needs to deposit and withdraw money many times a day and doesn't expect interest. Which account suits them?

   A company withdraws more than its account balance and pays interest on the extra. What form of bank lending is this?

   - [ ] Money at call
   - [ ] A term loan
   - [x] An overdraft
   - [ ] Cash credit

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   An overdraft: withdrawing more than your balance and paying interest on it.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="2ef1eec7" -->
   What is credit creation?

   - [ ] The central bank printing more naira
   - [x] A bank granting a loan by crediting the borrower's account, so deposits grow by a multiple of reserves
   - [ ] A customer depositing cash into a savings account
   - [ ] A bank buying treasury bills

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   When a bank lends, it creates a deposit of the same size in the borrower's account. Lent and re-deposited round after round, deposits grow to a multiple of the original reserves.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: gap id="f2c30904" answers="generating;mobilising|mobilizing;channelling|channeling" -->
   Fill in the gaps. Capital formation happens in three stages: ___ savings, ___ savings and ___ them into productive use.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Generating, mobilising and channelling.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: mcq id="55d176dd" -->
   Which of these is a function of the central bank, not of a commercial bank?

   - [x] Acting as lender of last resort to banks
   - [ ] Renting out safe deposit lockers

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Lender of last resort: the central bank is the banker to banks and lends to them in a crisis.

6. <!-- q: mcq id="399ef905" -->
