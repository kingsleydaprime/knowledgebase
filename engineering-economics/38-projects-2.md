# Projects 2: Tools, Roles, Charters and Contracts

Project management tools, roles, charters and contracts are what keep a project on time, on budget and to quality.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: b8527f9b-5333-4dd6-a25a-db7c7eec1742 -->

You should know what a project is and the levels of plan from [[engineering-economics/37-projects-1|Projects 1]].

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. describe the tools for planning, scheduling and control, and tell monitoring from evaluation;
2. describe the project manager's roles, and why communication matters most;
3. explain the project charter and the common contract types, and use the capital-budgeting terms from the 2017/2018 paper.

## What the tools are for
<!-- bite: d56162f0-d7f4-45c9-a838-00da3275e365 -->

Remember the three objectives from lesson 37: quality, time and cost? Every tool in this lesson exists to keep one of them on track. Some break the work down, some put it on a calendar, some say who does what, and some check progress against the plan.

> **Flag:** like lesson 37, this is general knowledge. Your lecture materials don't cover it.

## Tools for planning and scheduling
<!-- bite: 7aebf15d-5e7e-4c15-8b14-a47cf7e1d6b6 -->

- **Work breakdown structure (WBS):** breaks a large, complex project into smaller, more manageable units. A hostel splits into foundation, walls, roof, plumbing, electrics; each of those splits again.
- **Gantt (bar) chart:** shows activities against time; used to plan and report progress.
- **Responsibility (matrix) chart:** shows who is responsible for which task; useful when allocating tasks to different people in a team.
- **PERT and CPM:** network techniques for planning and control (lesson 35).

## Tools for control
<!-- bite: ae0e3288-fb32-4627-8007-c30121fe19a2 -->

- **Cost control:** the process of controlling cost-related items; it follows the cost management plan and the budget.
- **Monitoring:** a deliberate, systematic collection and analysis of information as the project progresses, to check efficiency and effectiveness. Performance is compared with the plan to find the **variance**.
- **Evaluation:** judges the worth of the activity.
- **Post-implementation review:** control begins early in planning and ends late with a thorough review of every step.

Monitoring asks "are we on plan?" while the work is going on. Evaluation asks "was it worth it?".

## The project manager's roles
<!-- bite: 08fb56f8-9dea-442b-8c5d-54a00aa4b7f7 -->

The project manager acts as a planner, an organiser, a coach and a leader (not a footballer!). **Leading** is a central role: working with and through others to achieve the project's objectives.

- **Staffing** means filling the project with qualified people who can take responsibility for specific parts.
- Managers also act as **motivator** (when the work is hard), negotiator and coordinator.
- They must practise the art of **integration** to build a good plan: pulling every part into one whole.
- **Functional management** controls resources and manages a business unit.

## Communication
<!-- bite: 3e58ff75-1293-4488-b545-8a7b37659cca -->

**Communication** is the most critical role for project success.

**Vertical communication** passes information on policies down and gives staff a way to respond up. The greater the complexity and uncertainty, the greater the need for **information**.

Most failed projects didn't fail for lack of engineering. Someone didn't know about a change, or a problem wasn't reported up in time.

## The project charter
<!-- bite: b7ba0cd5-c837-4177-8f19-d2e187527521 -->

The **project charter** formally authorises the project and gives it existence. It outlines the project manager's authority and refers to the project description.

The detailed scope statement is a separate document, not part of the charter.

## Contracts
<!-- bite: 1d9d1865-e5f4-4e7f-8afd-60e80ae77e5e -->

Common contract types:

1. **Fixed price (lump sum):** the seller agrees one price for the whole job. The seller carries the risk of overruns.
2. **Cost plus:** the buyer pays the seller's actual costs plus a fee. The buyer carries the risk.
3. **Time and materials:** the buyer pays for hours worked and materials used, at agreed rates.

The seller is also called the contractor, service provider or supplier. The buyer is the acquiring organisation.

## Terms from the capital-budgeting side 1
<!-- bite: 50f89190-c321-4a49-a3ac-d970a0a1cb45 -->

These appear in the 2017/2018 multiple-choice questions.

- **Capital budgeting (capital expenditure) decision:** the firm's long-term investment decision (lesson 35).
- **Assets** are grouped as current assets (cash, accounts receivable, inventory, prepaid expenses), fixed assets and investment assets. "Recurrent asset" is not a category.
- **Divestment:** cutting back or selling off part of an operation.
- **Total sales:** the full value of goods or services sold to third parties (cash plus credit).
- **Cost:** the outlay incurred to acquire an asset.

## Terms from the capital-budgeting side 2
<!-- bite: b420edae-8664-4aa8-a5d4-9db7fa29d3a8 -->

- **Time preference for money:** a person offered ₦1,000,000 now or later prefers it now, because money has time value (lesson 04).
- **Internal rate of return:** the rate that equates the present value of cash inflows with the present value of cash outflows (lesson 13).
- **Two criteria that fully recognise the timing of cash flows:** net present value and internal rate of return. The accounting rate of return and the payback period do not (lesson 14).

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: 5cf7c3ed-7286-4cba-8180-400ec646b770 -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: mcq id="a19dc9b8" -->
   Which tool breaks a large, complex project into smaller, more manageable units?

   - [x] Work breakdown structure
   - [ ] Gantt chart
   - [ ] Project charter
   - [ ] Responsibility chart

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The work breakdown structure (WBS).

   </details>

2. <!-- q: mcq id="997521d8" -->
   A team leader needs to show which engineer is responsible for each task on a project. Which chart suits best?

   - [ ] Gantt chart
   - [x] Responsibility (matrix) chart
   - [ ] Work breakdown structure
   - [ ] P/V chart

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The responsibility (matrix) chart, which shows who is responsible for which task.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="c8ef0f17" -->
   Which document formally authorises a project and outlines the project manager's authority?

   - [ ] The scope statement
   - [x] The project charter
   - [ ] The Gantt chart
   - [ ] The contract

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The project charter. The detailed scope statement is a separate document.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: mcq id="06b6aa10" -->
   Under which contract type does the seller carry the risk if the job costs more than expected?

   - [x] Fixed price (lump sum)
   - [ ] Cost plus
   - [ ] Time and materials

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Fixed price: the seller agreed one price, so any overrun comes out of the seller's profit. Under cost plus, the buyer pays actual costs.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: mcq id="2ef1eec8" -->
   Which of these is NOT a category of asset?

   - [ ] Current assets
   - [ ] Fixed assets
   - [ ] Investment assets
   - [x] Recurrent assets

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   "Recurrent asset" is not a category. Assets are current, fixed and investment assets.

   </details>

6. <!-- q: mcq id="f2c30905" -->
   Which is the most critical role of a project manager for project success?

   - [ ] Staffing
   - [x] Communication
   - [ ] Cost control
   - [ ] Negotiation

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Communication. The greater the complexity and uncertainty, the greater the need for information.

   </details>
