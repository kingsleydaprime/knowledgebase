# Cost Estimation 1: Why Estimate, and Judgement-Based Methods

Cost estimation is the systematic process of forecasting the financial resources needed to complete a project, produce a product or deliver a service over its life cycle.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: dae91c71-e5c3-4f26-9a62-cefdd6e06bd5 -->

You should know the kinds of cost from [[engineering-economics/02-cost-concepts-1|Cost Concepts 1]], and have finished part 4.

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. define cost estimation, say what it is used for, and what poor estimates lead to;
2. list the costs a project estimate must cover, and the three things that decide which technique to use;
3. use expert judgement and analogy estimation, with their advantages and limits.

## What cost estimation is
<!-- bite: 45d9b315-2424-4a8d-b751-823bd19fb493 -->

Every method in parts 2 to 4 needs numbers for first cost, yearly cost and salvage value. Cost estimation is how you get those numbers, so a weak estimate makes the whole analysis meaningless.

You can do a perfect present worth calculation, but if the first cost you put in was a guess that's 40% too low, the answer is perfectly wrong. Garbage in, garbage out.

**Definition:** cost estimation is the systematic process of forecasting the financial resources needed to complete a project, produce a product or deliver a service over its life cycle.

## What it is used for, and what goes wrong
<!-- bite: 47874929-aa86-4be4-8fb0-26fe89454057 -->

**It is used for:** project feasibility analysis, capital budgeting, comparing alternative projects, pricing and bidding, and budget control and cost management.

**Poor estimates lead to:** cost overruns (actual cost above the estimate), project abandonment and inefficient use of resources.

Abandoned projects are a familiar sight on Nigerian roads: a half-built flyover, a hospital with no roof. Many of them started with an estimate that was far too low.

## Costs to estimate
<!-- bite: f30ab31e-7e5f-4178-86b8-8cc563d8f4fd -->

1. **Initial (capital) cost:** land, equipment, installation, commissioning.
2. **Operating and maintenance (O&M) cost:** labour, energy and fuel, repairs and spare parts, insurance, administration.
3. **Fixed vs variable cost** (lesson 02).
4. **Direct vs indirect cost** (lesson 02).

Which technique you use depends on the **project stage**, the **data available** and the **accuracy you need**. They range from simple judgement to quantitative methods.

At the idea stage you might only need "about ₦500 million". Before signing a contract you need every bolt counted. So there are three families of technique: qualitative (A), quantitative (B, lesson 20) and detailed (C, lesson 20).

## A1. Expert judgement
<!-- bite: d9175061-2054-473e-b188-36dc80855ccf -->

You rely on the experience and intuition of experts: consult experienced engineers or managers, use historical knowledge, and adjust for the situation.

**Example:** an engineer estimates a small water treatment plant at about ₦450M from similar past projects.

- **Advantages:** fast, cheap, useful when data is scarce.
- **Limits:** very subjective, prone to bias and optimism.

## A2. Analogy (comparative) estimation
<!-- bite: 1118a1ee-488c-464b-b69c-2458cd219731 -->

You estimate by comparing with similar completed projects. You take the cost of the similar project and scale it by how the new one differs:

$$\text{Estimated cost} = \text{Cost of similar project} \times \text{adjustment factor}$$

**Example:** a 5 km road cost ₦1M, so a 7 km road is estimated at 1M × 7/5 = ₦1.4M. The adjustment factor is 7/5.

- **Advantages:** simple, useful in early planning.
- **Limits:** accuracy depends on how similar the projects are, and it ignores technology change.

Both of these lean on someone's judgement. The next lesson's techniques replace judgement with formulas built from data, which makes them more objective.

1. <!-- q: gap id="0864f293" answers="forecasting;life cycle|lifecycle" -->
   Fill in the gaps. Cost estimation is the systematic process of ___ the financial resources needed to complete a project, produce a product or deliver a service over its ___.

2. <!-- q: exact id="895d4fdb" answer="200" accept="₦200M|200M|₦200 million|200 million" -->
   A 4 km water pipeline cost ₦80 million. Using analogy estimation, what would a similar 10 km pipeline cost, in millions of naira?

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   80 × 10/4 = ₦200 million. The adjustment factor is 10/4 = 2.5.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="b67854a8" -->
   Which three things decide which estimation technique you use?

   - [ ] The interest rate, the inflation rate and the tax rate
   - [x] The project stage, the data available and the accuracy you need
   - [ ] The client, the contractor and the consultant
   - [ ] The first cost, the salvage value and the life

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The project stage, the data available and the accuracy you need.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: mcq id="950e5b94" -->
   A new type of plant has never been built before and there is almost no cost data. Which technique is most useful at the idea stage?

   - [x] Expert judgement
   - [ ] Detailed (bottom-up) estimation
   - [ ] Capacity factoring with a known exponent

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Expert judgement: fast, cheap and useful when data is scarce. Detailed estimation needs a detailed design, and capacity factoring needs data on similar plants.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: mcq id="57a3ce7b" -->
   What is a cost overrun?

   - [ ] When a project finishes early
   - [x] When the actual cost is above the estimate
   - [ ] When the estimate includes a contingency
   - [ ] When the project is abandoned

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   A cost overrun is when the actual cost is above the estimate. It is one of the three results of poor estimates, with project abandonment and inefficient use of resources.

   </details>

6. <!-- q: open id="c8fcd474" -->
   Give one advantage and one limitation each of expert judgement and analogy estimation.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Expert judgement is fast, cheap and useful when data is scarce, but it is very subjective and prone to bias and optimism. Analogy estimation is simple and useful in early planning, but its accuracy depends on how similar the projects are, and it ignores technology change.

   </details>
