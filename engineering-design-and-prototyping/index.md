# Engineering Design and Prototyping

## What engineering design and prototyping is

Engineering design and prototyping is how engineers go from a human need to a working solution: first designing the solution, then building models of it to test whether it actually works.

Every product you use went through it. Someone noticed a problem (bags that hurt your back, hands that take too long to dry), came up with many possible answers, picked the best one, worked out every detail, built a rough model, tested it, and went back to fix what failed. This subject teaches each of those steps and the tools engineers use for them.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end you will be able to:
- define a design problem clearly, with requirements you can actually test;
- come up with many different ideas for solving it, using structured techniques;
- choose between ideas fairly, with scoring tables instead of gut feeling;
- improve the chosen idea and pin down its dimensions, materials and parts;
- judge what makes a design good: how it performs, its whole life from mine to dump, and the rules and people it answers to;
- plan prototypes and tests, and find how a design could fail before it does.

## Key ideas to remember

Once you've read the lessons, these are the points worth keeping. Each links to where it's explained.

1. **The design process is a loop, not a line.** When testing shows a problem, you go back to an earlier phase. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/02-the-design-process|02]]
2. **Requirements say what it must do, constraints limit how, and criteria for success say how you'll know it worked.** → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/04-problem-definition|04]]
3. **A good requirement has a number you can test.** "Strong" is weak; "holds at least 4 textbooks" is good. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/05-requirements-and-requirement-writing|05]]
4. **Generate many different ideas before judging any.** Judging too early kills them. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/06-concept-generation-1|06]]
5. **Weighted totals are weight × score, added up.** Show the working, because even lecture slides get it wrong. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/11-concept-selection-3|11]]
6. **Basic needs come before delight.** A delightful product that misses a basic need still fails. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/10-concept-selection-2|10]]
7. **Most of a product's impact can come from how it's used, not what it's made of.** → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/15-considerations-of-good-design-3|15]]
8. **Find failures on paper, where they're cheap.** FMEA ranks them by severity × occurrence × detection. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/19-failure-mode-and-effects-analysis|19]]
