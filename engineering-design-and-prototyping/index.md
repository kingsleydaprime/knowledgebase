# Engineering Design and Prototyping

## What engineering design and prototyping is

Engineering design and prototyping is how engineers go from a human need to a working solution: first designing the solution, then building models of it to test whether it actually works.

Every product you use went through it. Someone noticed a problem (bags that hurt your back, hands that take too long to dry), came up with many possible answers, picked the best one, worked out every detail, built a rough model, tested it, and went back to fix what failed. This subject teaches each of those steps and the tools engineers use for them.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end you will be able to:
- find everyone a design affects, and turn their needs into requirements you can actually test and trace;
- come up with many different ideas for solving it, using structured techniques;
- choose between ideas fairly, with scoring tables instead of gut feeling;
- improve the chosen idea, and break it into subsystems with clear interfaces;
- judge what makes a design good: how it performs, its whole life from mine to dump, and the rules and people it answers to;
- detail it in CAD, check it by simulation, and add the electronics that make it smart;
- prototype it, find how it could fail or hurt someone, work out how reliable it is, and prove it works;
- plan how it will be made, and report it like a professional.

## Key ideas to remember

Once you've read the lessons, these are the points worth keeping. Each links to where it's explained.

1. **The design process is a loop, not a line.** When testing shows a problem, you go back to an earlier phase. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/02-the-design-process|02]]
2. **Requirements say what it must do, constraints limit how, and criteria for success say how you'll know it worked.** → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/04-problem-definition|04]]
3. **A good requirement has a number you can test.** "Strong" is weak; "holds at least 4 textbooks" is good. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/06-requirements-and-requirement-writing|06]]
4. **Generate many different ideas before judging any.** Judging too early kills them. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/08-concept-generation-1|08]]
5. **Weighted totals are weight × score, added up.** Show the working, because even lecture slides get it wrong. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/13-concept-selection-3|13]]
6. **Basic needs come before delight.** A delightful product that misses a basic need still fails. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/12-concept-selection-2|12]]
7. **Most of a product's impact can come from how it's used, not what it's made of.** → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/19-considerations-of-good-design-3|19]]
8. **Find failures on paper, where they're cheap.** FMEA ranks them by severity × occurrence × detection. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/27-failure-mode-and-effects-analysis|27]]
9. **Parts in series multiply their reliabilities,** so every extra part lowers reliability; parallel spares raise it. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/28-reliability|28]]
10. **Design the hazard out before you reach for a warning label.** → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/29-safety-and-risk|29]]
11. **Verification: built right. Validation: built the right thing.** A design can pass every requirement and still fail its users. → [[engineering-design-and-prototyping/30-verification-validation-and-testing|30]]
