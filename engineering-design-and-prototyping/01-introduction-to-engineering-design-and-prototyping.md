# Introduction to Engineering Design and Prototyping

Engineering design and prototyping is how we go from a human need to a working solution.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: 085849a3-0441-4305-8e7b-b58ec0fb8253 -->

Nothing beyond having used everyday products: a phone, a bag, a water bottle.

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. say what engineering design and engineering prototyping are, and how they depend on each other;
2. list four things design and prototyping enable engineers to do;
3. explain why design and prototyping are challenging, and the factors that make them complex.

## What engineering design and prototyping are
<!-- bite: 7106d4f3-8060-4375-9a21-de7b2c9ca174 -->

This course is about how we go from a human need to a working solution. This first lesson gives the two big ideas (design and prototyping), and the next one gives the steps the rest of the course follows.

- **Engineering design** is the process of creating solutions to meet human needs and desires by applying scientific and engineering knowledge, skills and tools.
- **Engineering prototyping** is the process of creating physical or virtual models of the design solution to test its feasibility, functionality and usability.

So design is where the solution is created, and prototyping is where we find out if it actually works.

## Where they sit: systems engineering
<!-- bite: 86b19951-905a-4347-ae93-f9deb826e70d -->

According to the lecture pdf, **systems engineering** is the discipline of designing, developing and managing complex systems that satisfy the needs of customers and stakeholders.

Design and prototyping sit inside it, and they depend on each other: design creates the solution, prototyping tests it. A design you never test is a guess. A prototype with no design behind it is just a model of nothing in particular.

A one-sentence version for an exam: engineering design creates solutions to human needs using scientific and engineering knowledge, and engineering prototyping builds physical or virtual models of that solution to test its feasibility, functionality and usability.

## Importance
<!-- bite: e3869de9-01e2-4f43-84c6-8f5251fb7de1 -->

Why do we bother with all this? Because design and prototyping enable engineers to:

1. Solve real-world problems that affect people, society and the environment.
2. Innovate new products, services and systems that create value and improve quality of life.
3. Communicate and collaborate with other engineers, customers and stakeholders effectively.
4. Learn from feedback and failures and improve their design solutions continuously.

Number 4 is the one to remember. A prototype that fails a test is not a waste. It is the cheapest place to find out you were wrong.

## Challenges
<!-- bite: e1243ea2-3b6d-4946-89a1-7704c8358c1a -->

But it's not easy. Design and prototyping are challenging because they involve:

1. Dealing with ill-defined, complex and dynamic problems that have multiple and conflicting objectives, requirements, constraints and criteria for success.
2. Generating and selecting among many possible design concepts that have different trade-offs, risks and uncertainties.
3. Refining and detailing the design concept to meet the specifications and standards of the system.
4. Prototyping and testing the design solution in realistic conditions and evaluating its performance and outcomes.
5. Managing the resources, time and cost of the design and prototyping process.

Notice that each challenge lines up with a step of the process. That is not an accident, and the next lesson shows those steps.

## Why design and prototyping are complex
<!-- bite: 773c3dd1-90e9-4dc4-80cf-9669673fe10c -->

An exam can also ask *why* it is complex. These are the factors behind the challenges above:

1. Problems are ill-defined and change as you learn more.
2. Objectives, requirements and constraints are many and often conflict (for example light but strong, strong but cheap).
3. Many stakeholders want different things.
4. There are many possible concepts, each with trade-offs, risks and uncertainties.
5. The parts of a system depend on each other, so a change in one affects the others.
6. Several disciplines are involved (mechanical, electrical, software, materials, human factors).

## Why it is complex: the remaining factors
<!-- bite: 8b8fda76-d635-42fa-b4e1-8c4437c7a811 -->

The list carries on:

7. Uncertainty about performance in realistic conditions, only found out by prototyping and testing.
8. Limits on resources, time and cost.
9. Rules to follow: standards, regulations, ethics and safety.

Take a school bag. The student wants it light, the parent wants it strong, the shop wants it cheap. Light, strong and cheap pull in different directions (factors 2 and 3), and you only really know how it survives a rainy season after you test it (factor 7).

So how do engineers handle all this without getting lost? They follow a process, one step at a time. That's the next lesson.

## Common pitfalls
<!-- bite: 92ea141c-abe8-47a2-a4f3-c787ffd508fc -->

1. **Mixing up challenges and complexity.** Challenges are *what* is hard (selecting concepts, testing, managing cost). Complexity factors are *why* it is hard (conflicting requirements, many stakeholders, parts that depend on each other). An exam question on one wants that one.
2. **Saying prototyping is only physical.** A prototype can be virtual too: a CAD model or a simulation counts.

## Check your understanding
<!-- bite: 09b42d53-fb34-4264-a273-290870147a86 -->

Try each one before you open the answer.

1. <!-- q: gap id="a1c4e702" answers="feasibility;functionality;usability" -->
   Fill in the gaps. Engineering prototyping creates physical or virtual models of the design solution to test its ___, ___ and ___.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Feasibility, functionality and usability.

   </details>

2. <!-- q: mcq id="5b9e0f31" -->
   A team builds a computer simulation of a new bridge to see if it can carry the expected traffic. Is this prototyping?

   - [x] Yes, a prototype can be a virtual model
   - [ ] No, a prototype must be a physical model
   - [ ] No, this is detailed design

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Yes. Prototyping creates physical *or virtual* models of the design to test it, and a simulation is a virtual model.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="e7d21a58" -->
   Which of these are things design and prototyping enable engineers to do? Choose all that apply.

   - [x] Solve real-world problems that affect people, society and the environment
   - [x] Learn from feedback and failures and improve continuously
   - [x] Communicate and collaborate with customers and stakeholders
   - [ ] Avoid having to follow standards and regulations

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The first three. The fourth item on the real list is innovating new products, services and systems. Standards and regulations still apply; they are one of the reasons design is complex.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: mcq id="3f0b6c94" -->
   A phone maker wants a thinner phone, but a thinner phone has less room for the battery, and users want the battery to last longer. Which complexity factor is this?

   - [ ] Several disciplines are involved
   - [x] Objectives and requirements conflict
   - [ ] Rules to follow: standards and regulations

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Conflicting objectives and requirements. Thin and long battery life pull in opposite directions, so the designer has to trade one against the other.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: open id="9c62d8e1" -->
   Explain how engineering design and engineering prototyping depend on each other.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Design creates the solution and prototyping tests it. Without prototyping, a design is never checked for feasibility, functionality and usability. Without design, there is nothing to prototype. The test results then feed back into the design so it improves.

   </details>
