# Case Studies

A case study is a real product described from its mission to its future, to show how design and prototyping solve real needs.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: 24de46cd-f38d-43e3-b0a8-c344c72284b6 -->

You should know the seven phases of the design process ([[engineering-design-and-prototyping/02-the-design-process|lesson 02]]).

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. state a case study in the right order: mission, capabilities, impact, future;
2. describe five famous designs that way.

## What a case study is
<!-- bite: 2da83d70-c944-4359-bc82-911e4548b910 -->

We've talked about design in general. Now let's see it in real products people actually use.

A case study takes one real product and tells its story. When stating a case study, start with:

1. **The mission** of the project: the need it was made to meet.
2. **The capabilities**: what it can do.
3. **The impact**: what changed because of it.
4. **The future**: where it goes next.

Remember this order. It's the same order as the design process: the mission is the problem definition, the capabilities are the solution, the impact is the testing in the real world, and the future is the next iteration.

## Mars Rover
<!-- bite: 8b1e263f-9d36-4aab-b044-ef1273d80ff4 -->

- **Mission:** Explore the Martian surface, search for signs of past or present life, and study the planet's geology and climate.
- **Capabilities:** Cameras, spectrometers and drills to analyse rocks, weather patterns and the Martian atmosphere.
- **Impact:** Revealed evidence of ancient water, diverse minerals and potential for supporting microbial life.
- **Future:** Paves the way for future robotic and human missions, and gives data for a possible Martian base.

## iPhone
<!-- bite: 4a27193b-8037-428f-95c1-28695e601b2d -->

- **Mission:** Revolutionise mobile communication.
- **Capabilities:** Touchscreen interface, app store, internet, camera and multimedia in one device.
- **Impact:** Transformed communication, entertainment and business, and shaped online behaviour.
- **Future:** New models yearly with better cameras, processing power and features.

## Tesla Model 3
<!-- bite: 21f284f3-7186-4f8b-8da8-4ee92b4ac656 -->

- **Mission:** Make electric vehicles more affordable, powerful and stylish.
- **Capabilities:** Strong acceleration, long driving range, advanced driver-assistance.
- **Impact:** Pushed traditional carmakers to invest heavily in electric vehicles.
- **Future:** Charging infrastructure and range anxiety are still hurdles; better batteries and charging networks should help.

## EpiPen
<!-- bite: 561146cf-2520-48cd-aadb-d2844c871ef5 -->

- **Mission:** Deliver a precise dose of epinephrine within seconds to treat anaphylactic shock (a severe allergic reaction).
- **Capabilities:** Compact and easy to carry, so people with allergies can manage their condition confidently.
- **Impact:** Increased awareness of allergies and of carrying an EpiPen.
- **Future:** Next-generation EpiPens with improved safety features, possibly treating other conditions.

## Dyson Airblade
<!-- bite: 9ee69bd3-d096-41ec-ac9b-d5eb9301422d -->

- **Mission:** Dry hands quickly and hygienically.
- **Capabilities:** Uses high-velocity air.
- **Impact:** Reduces the spread of bacteria compared to conventional dryers, and uses less energy.
- **Future:** Could become the preferred hand dryer in more public and private places as awareness of hygiene and sustainability grows.

Every one of these started with a clear mission. That's why the course starts there too: the next lesson is problem definition.

## Common pitfalls
<!-- bite: 158a5a95-8793-4e76-9886-c5c0793a5970 -->

1. **Starting with the features.** "The iPhone has a touchscreen" is a capability. Start with the mission, the need it was made for.
2. **Forgetting the future.** A case study isn't finished at the impact. Say what comes next, including the problems still unsolved (like range anxiety for the Tesla).

   Fill in the gaps. When stating a case study, give (i) the ___, (ii) the ___, (iii) the ___, (iv) the ___.

   - [ ] Mission
   - [ ] Capabilities
   - [x] Impact
   - [ ] Future

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Impact: what changed in the world because of the product.

   </details>

   Which product's mission was to deliver a precise dose of epinephrine within seconds to treat anaphylactic shock?

   - [ ] Dyson Airblade
   - [x] EpiPen
   - [ ] Mars Rover

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The EpiPen. Anaphylactic shock is a severe allergic reaction.

   </details>

4. <!-- q: open id="d9415f7e" -->
   Write a short case study for a product you use every day (not one from this lesson), using the four parts in order.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Any product works if all four parts are there in order. For example, a solar lantern. Mission: give homes light when there is no electricity. Capabilities: a solar panel charges a battery in the day, and LED bulbs light the room at night. Impact: children can read at night, and families spend less on kerosene, which is also safer. Future: brighter lights, phone charging, and cheaper batteries.

   </details>
