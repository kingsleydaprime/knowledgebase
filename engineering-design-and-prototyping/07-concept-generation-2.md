# Concept Generation 2: SCAMPER

SCAMPER is a creative problem-solving technique that stimulates new ideas by asking seven questions about an existing design or concept.

## Before you start
<!-- bite: f8cb89d9-b772-4a8d-b37c-d6681836a16c -->

You should know what concept generation is and why diverse concepts matter ([[engineering-design-and-prototyping/06-concept-generation-1|Concept Generation 1]]).

After this lesson you will be able to:

1. say what each letter of SCAMPER stands for, with the question it asks;
2. give the benefits of SCAMPER and the tips for using it;
3. apply SCAMPER step by step to improve an existing product.

## What SCAMPER is
<!-- bite: 57eb9f56-b0e9-44fb-b57a-fdd135434dab -->

Brainstorming says "give me ideas". That's hard when your head is empty. SCAMPER makes it easier: instead of one big question, it gives you seven small ones to ask about something that already exists.

The letters stand for **S**ubstitute, **C**ombine, **A**dapt, **M**odify (Magnify and Minify), **P**ut to another use, **E**liminate, **R**earrange/Reverse. It stimulates creative thinking by asking questions about an existing design or concept.

SCAMPER shows up in two phases: problem definition (asking questions helps you understand the problem) and concept generation (the answers are new concepts).

## The letters: S, C and A
<!-- bite: 8789a6f6-24a6-4bf9-b455-388d38f17f0c -->

| Letter | What it means | Question to ask | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S: Substitute | Replace parts, materials, people or processes | What can be used instead? | Use a cheaper material than the one now used |
| C: Combine | Merge two or more ideas, objects or functions | What can be combined? | Combine two features of existing products to create something new |
| A: Adapt | Modify an existing idea or object for a new purpose | What can be adapted? | Adapt a technology from one industry to solve a problem in another |

## The letters: M, P, E and R
<!-- bite: 279d1e15-ee12-42bc-a8d8-f659c2ae4bca -->

| Letter | What it means | Question to ask | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M: Modify (Magnify and Minify) | Change the size, shape, color or other properties | What can be modified? | Make the product bigger or smaller, change its color or shape |
| P: Put to another use | Find new ways to use existing objects or processes | What else can this be used for? | Use the product in a new and unexpected way |
| E: Eliminate | Remove unnecessary parts, features or processes | What can be eliminated? | Simplify the design by removing unnecessary features |
| R: Rearrange/Reverse | Change the order or arrangement of things | What can be rearranged or reversed? | Change the order of steps in a process, or reverse the direction of something |

## Benefits and tips
<!-- bite: a1d57d1c-6d6e-47ef-80c5-827ab2c3cd27 -->

**Benefits of SCAMPER:**

1. Promotes creative thinking.
2. Easy to use.
3. Encourages collaboration.
4. Improves problem solving.

**Tips for using SCAMPER:**

1. Start with a clear problem statement.
2. Don't judge your ideas.
3. Build on other people's ideas.
4. Be persistent.

Notice tip 2 is the same rule as brainstorming. Judging waits for concept selection.

## Applying SCAMPER: the systematic steps
<!-- bite: 8742369a-691d-4672-9f2e-e8981f418a84 -->

Knowing the letters isn't enough. Here is how to actually run it on a product:

1. Pick the existing product or system and write a clear problem statement (what do you want to improve?).
2. List its parts, functions and users.
3. Go through S, C, A, M, P, E, R one letter at a time and ask the question for each. Write every idea, without judging.
4. Build on each other's ideas (work in a group if you can).
5. Screen the ideas against the criteria for success (cost, feasibility, safety, functionality).
6. Pick the best ideas, sketch or prototype them, test, and repeat if needed.

**Practical tips:** start with a clear problem statement, don't judge ideas early, build on other people's ideas, be persistent, and use the user's complaints as a source of questions.

## Example: improving an ordinary backpack
<!-- bite: 6a64ad9d-666f-4a6b-93a9-727e9fdd1ca8 -->

Remember the backpack from problem definition? Students said the straps dig in, everything gets mixed up, and the laptop is hard to reach. Here is SCAMPER on it, one letter at a time:

| Letter | Question | Idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substitute | What can be used instead? | Recycled fabric instead of virgin nylon |
| Combine | What can be combined? | Add a built-in power bank and charging cable |
| Adapt | What can be adapted? | Use the load-spreading harness of hiking packs |
| Modify | What can be made bigger, smaller or changed? | Slimmer shape, brighter colour for night visibility |
| Put to another use | What else can it be used for? | Converts into a picnic or sitting mat |
| Eliminate | What can be removed? | Remove extra zips and straps to cut weight and cost |
| Rearrange or reverse | What can be rearranged? | Laptop compartment on the back so it is reachable without removing the bag |

Seven questions, seven ideas. Step 5 then screens them: the power bank adds cost and a safety question, the hiking harness fixes the strap complaint directly.

## SCAMPER in real engineering
<!-- bite: d784120d-8432-4d43-84e7-dd7ffa316496 -->

SCAMPER isn't just for bags. You can see its letters in real products:

- **Substitute:** aluminium drink cans replaced heavier steel ones.
- **Combine:** the phone combined a camera, a music player and a map in one device (remember the iPhone case study).
- **Adapt:** Velcro adapted the way burrs stick to animal fur.
- **Eliminate:** wireless earbuds eliminated the cable.

That's how it enhances innovation: it forces you to look at every part of a product from seven angles, so ideas come even when you're stuck. The next lesson finishes concept generation with three techniques that borrow ideas from outside: from nature, from other fields, and from crowds.

## Common pitfalls
<!-- bite: 3e1f65d2-dd66-46e5-92e4-4966d9e08bfe -->

1. **"M" is only "make it bigger".** Modify covers Magnify *and* Minify, and also shape, colour and other properties.
2. **Skipping the problem statement.** Without it, the ideas have nothing to aim at, and step 5 can't screen them.
3. **Stopping at the ideas.** SCAMPER ends with screening, prototyping and testing, not with a list.

   Fill in the gaps. SCAMPER stands for S: ___, C: ___, A: ___, M: ___, P: ___, E: ___, R: ___.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify (Magnify and Minify), Put to another use, Eliminate, Rearrange/Reverse.

2. <!-- q: mcq id="9a2f5c18" -->
   A designer takes an umbrella and asks "What else could this be used for?" and comes up with a walking stick. Which letter is this?

   - [ ] A: Adapt
   - [x] P: Put to another use
   - [ ] C: Combine

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Put to another use: finding new ways to use an existing object.

   </details>

3. <!-- q: mcq id="e3b06d71" -->
   A car maker removes the spare tyre and gives drivers a puncture repair kit instead, to save weight and boot space. Which letter fits best?

   - [x] E: Eliminate
   - [ ] M: Modify
   - [ ] R: Rearrange/Reverse

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Eliminate: removing an unnecessary part. (You could also argue Substitute, since the kit replaces the tyre, which is fine, but the main idea is removing the heavy part.)

   </details>

4. <!-- q: mcq id="0d8c4a37" -->
   Which of these are benefits of SCAMPER given in the lecture? Choose all that apply.

   - [x] Promotes creative thinking
   - [x] Easy to use
   - [x] Encourages collaboration
   - [ ] Gives one correct answer

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   The first three. The fourth benefit is that it improves problem solving. SCAMPER gives many ideas, not one correct answer.

   </details>

5. <!-- q: open id="f71b2e95" -->
   Apply SCAMPER to an ordinary plastic bucket. Give one idea for each of any four letters.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   For example. Substitute: a fold-flat silicone bucket instead of rigid plastic. Combine: add measuring marks so it is also a measuring jug. Modify: a wider base so it doesn't tip over. Eliminate: remove the metal handle and mould a handle into the rim. Any idea works if it answers that letter's question.

   </details>

   List the systematic steps for applying SCAMPER to improve an existing product.

   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   (1) Pick the product and write a clear problem statement. (2) List its parts, functions and users. (3) Go through S, C, A, M, P, E, R one at a time, writing every idea without judging. (4) Build on each other's ideas. (5) Screen the ideas against the criteria for success. (6) Pick the best, sketch or prototype them, test, and repeat if needed.

   </details>
