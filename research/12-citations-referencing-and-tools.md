# Citations, Referencing & Tools

**[reference]** · **⏳ outline — deep note in progress** (part of the research-course build).

The mechanics that make a paper *look and function* like real scholarship — crediting prior work correctly, and the toolchain (LaTeX, BibTeX, reference managers) the field actually uses.

## Will cover
- **Why cite** — credit (avoiding [[research/07-research-ethics-and-integrity|plagiarism]]), evidence for claims, situating your work, letting readers dig deeper.
- **How to cite well** — cite the *original* source not a secondary one, quote sparingly vs paraphrase, cite claims not just names, don't over- or under-cite; when to cite (any claim that isn't yours or common knowledge).
- **Citation styles** — numeric (IEEE, `[1]`) vs author-year (APA, `(Smith, 2020)`); in-text vs footnote; matching the venue's required style.
- **Reference managers** — **Zotero** (free, standard), Mendeley, Papers; browser capture, PDF library, auto-generating bibliographies, syncing your reading notes ([[research/02-reading-papers|reading papers]]).
- **LaTeX & BibTeX/BibLaTeX** — why STEM writes in LaTeX (math, references, consistent formatting), **Overleaf** for collaborative editing, `.bib` files, `\cite{}`, and letting the toolchain format everything.
- **Word + Zotero** — the non-LaTeX path, for fields/venues that use it.
- **Avoiding accidental plagiarism** — paraphrase properly, track sources as you write, quote when using exact words, self-citation for your own prior work.
