# Paper Types & Writing Styles

**[reference]** · **⏳ outline — deep note in progress** (part of the research-course build). **This is the "different writing styles" note** — the one you specifically wanted, since you'll write across AI/ML, robotics, chips, and possibly other fields, each with its own conventions.

There is no single "research paper" template. The **structure, tone, evidence, and length shift by *paper type* and by *field*** — writing an empirical ML paper like a math-theory paper (or vice-versa) reads as amateur. This note is how to recognize which register a piece of work needs and switch into it.

## Will cover

### By *paper type*
- **Empirical / experimental** (most ML) — hypothesis → method → experiments → results dominate; heavy on baselines, ablations, tables; "we show empirically that…".
- **Systems / engineering** — the *artifact* is the contribution; emphasis on design, architecture, trade-offs, and benchmarks (throughput/latency/cost); "we built X and evaluated it."
- **Theory / mathematical** — definitions → theorems → proofs; terse, formal, every claim proven; experiments optional or illustrative.
- **Robotics / hardware** — hybrid: system design + real-world experiments + often a video/demo; sim-to-real gap discussion; safety.
- **Survey / review** — no new experiments; contribution is *organization* — a taxonomy of a field, thematic synthesis, open problems.
- **Position / vision** — argue *for* a viewpoint or research direction; persuasion over data.
- **Short paper / workshop / extended abstract** — one crisp idea, less complete evidence; a great first-paper target.
- **Reproducibility / negative-results** — replicate or refute; increasingly valued.

### By *field* (conventions differ)
- **CS/ML** — **conference**-first culture, arXiv preprints, 8-page limits, code release expected, fast cycle, related-work often late.
- **Traditional engineering / physics** — **journal**-first, IEEE/Elsevier styles, longer, more formal, methods-heavy.
- **Sciences (bio/chem)** — rigid IMRaD (Intro-Methods-Results-and-Discussion), passive voice still common.
- **Humanities/social** — argumentative essays, qualitative evidence, footnote-heavy citation styles.

### The transferable move
- How to **infer the target style**: read 3–5 recent papers *from your exact target venue* and mirror their structure, length, tone, and citation style before writing a word.
