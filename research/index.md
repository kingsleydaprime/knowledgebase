# Research and Paper Writing

Research is finding out something nobody knew yet, and writing it up so clearly that other people can check it and build on it. This subject teaches you how to do both: how to find a good question, run a fair study, and write the paper.

You meet research everywhere at university: a final-year project, a seminar paper, a SIWES report that has to say what you found, a lecturer's paper you're told to read. The same skills work whatever the field, whether it's machine learning, robotics, chip design or something you got curious about last week.

**By the end you'll be able to:** read a research paper quickly and critically; turn curiosity into a sharp, answerable question; design a study whose results you can trust; analyse them honestly; and write a paper a reviewer can follow, in the style your field expects.

**And you'll have done it.** This subject is hands-on: every lesson moves one real study forward, and by the end you'll have finished a small piece of research and written it up as a short paper. The lessons follow a default study you can do with just a phone, "How accurate are free weather apps for Lagos?", or you can bring your own question.

**Where to start:** lesson 01, What Research Is. Then read the course in order: part A is doing the research, part B is writing it, part C is getting it out.

## Part A: Doing the research

1. [[research/01-what-research-is|01 — What Research Is]]: the mindset, what a "contribution" means, and the kinds of research (empirical, theoretical, systems, qualitative).
2. [[research/02-reading-papers|02 — Reading Papers]]: the three-pass method, reading critically, and mapping a field.
3. [[research/03-finding-a-problem-and-literature-review|03 — Finding a Problem and the Literature Review]]: spotting a real gap, and doing a literature review.
4. [[research/04-research-questions-and-hypotheses|04 — Research Questions and Hypotheses]]: framing a good, scoped, answerable question; hypotheses and falsifiability.
5. [[research/05-methodology-and-experiment-design|05 — Methodology and Experiment Design]]: sound design, baselines, ablations, controls, threats to validity, reproducibility.
6. [[research/06-analyzing-and-interpreting-results|06 — Analysing and Interpreting Results]]: honest analysis, statistical significance, negative results, over-claiming.
7. [[research/07-research-ethics-and-integrity|07 — Research Ethics and Integrity]]: plagiarism, p-hacking, authorship, data ethics, open science.

## Part B: Writing the paper

8. [[research/08-anatomy-of-a-paper|08 — The Anatomy of a Paper]]: section by section, from abstract to conclusion, and the argument underneath it.
9. [[research/09-scientific-writing-craft|09 — Scientific Writing Craft]]: clarity, precision, the story and the claim, and the common mistakes.
10. Figures, Tables and Presenting Data: figures that carry the argument, and honest visualisation. *(Being written.)*
11. Paper Types and Writing Styles: empirical, systems, theory, hardware, survey and position papers, and how fields differ. *(Being written.)*
12. Citations, Referencing and Tools: citation styles, BibTeX and Zotero, LaTeX and Overleaf, avoiding plagiarism. *(Being written.)*

## Part C: Getting it out

13. Venues and the Publication Process: preprints, conferences and journals, and choosing where to submit. *(Being written.)*
14. Peer Review and Rebuttals: how review works, reading your reviews, writing a rebuttal, being a reviewer. *(Being written.)*
15. Presenting and Defending: talks, posters, and defending your work. *(Being written.)*

## How to actually learn this

Reading this subject won't make you a researcher; doing research will. The path that works: read part A, then pick a tiny question and actually investigate it (even a small reproduction of a paper you like). Read part B as you write it up, and part C when you're ready to share it; a preprint or a workshop paper is a good first target. One real, finished, imperfect paper teaches more than ten lessons.
