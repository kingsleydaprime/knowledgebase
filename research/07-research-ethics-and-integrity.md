# Research Ethics & Integrity

**[reference]** · **⏳ outline — deep note in progress** (part of the research-course build).

Research runs entirely on **trust** — the whole edifice assumes people report what actually happened. This note is the non-negotiables, the grey areas, and how to stay on the right side of both.

## Will cover
- **The cardinal sins (FFP)** — **Fabrication** (making up data), **Falsification** (doctoring it), **Plagiarism** (passing off others' words/ideas as yours) — career-ending, no exceptions.
- **The subtle, common misconduct** — **p-hacking** (torturing data until it's "significant"), **HARKing** (hypothesizing after results are known), cherry-picking runs, salami-slicing one study into many papers, misleading figures.
- **Authorship & credit** — who earns it (and who doesn't — gift/ghost authorship), author order conventions by field, acknowledging contributions and funding, conflicts of interest.
- **Citing properly** — crediting prior work, quoting vs paraphrasing, self-plagiarism, and **AI-tool disclosure** (using LLMs to draft/edit — the emerging norms).
- **Human & animal subjects** — informed consent, IRB/ethics-board approval, privacy/data protection (esp. relevant for HCI, medical, and data-heavy work).
- **Data & code ethics** — dataset licensing/consent, dual-use concerns, environmental cost, responsible release.
- **Open science** — preprints, open data/code, why transparency *is* integrity.
