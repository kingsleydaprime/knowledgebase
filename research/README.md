# Research & Paper Writing

How to **do research** and **write research papers** — a meta-skill that powers every other domain in this vault. Learn it once and it applies whether you're researching AI/ML, robotics, chip design, or something you got curious about last week. Built **balanced**: the universal research process + writing craft as the spine, with **different fields' and paper types' conventions called out** (so you can write an empirical ML paper, a systems paper, or a survey and hit the right register each time).

**Source:** distilled from the canon of "how to do research" guidance — *The Craft of Research* (Booth, Colomb & Williams), Simon Peyton Jones' "How to Write a Great Research Paper," the "three-pass" paper-reading method (Keshav), *Writing for Computer Science* (Zobel), and standard scientific-method / peer-review practice. `[reference]` — a skill you learn by **doing** (reading real papers, writing a real one), not just reading about.

**Every note opens with a plain-language "kid version" before going deep, and ends with a Key insight.** Grouped into three parts.

## How to actually learn this

Reading this course won't make you a researcher — **doing** will. The path that works: read Part A, then **pick a tiny question and actually investigate it** (even a small reproduction of a paper you like); read Part B as you **write it up**; read Part C when you're ready to share it (a preprint or a workshop paper is a great first target). One real, finished, imperfect paper teaches more than ten notes.
