# Scientific Writing Craft

**[reference]** · **⏳ outline — deep note in progress** (part of the research-course build). Built around Zobel's *Writing for Computer Science* and Williams' *Style*.

Clear scientific writing isn't about big words — it's about making a complex idea **effortless to follow**. The craft of getting the idea from your head into the reader's with minimum friction.

## Will cover
- **Clarity over cleverness** — the goal is to be *understood*, not to sound smart; the reader's time is sacred.
- **One idea per paragraph, point first** — topic sentence leads; the paragraph defends it (the "reverse pyramid" again).
- **Precision** — concrete, measurable claims over vague ones ("reduces error by 12%" not "greatly improves"); define terms once, use them consistently.
- **Sentence-level craft** — prefer active voice and strong verbs, cut hedging and filler, short sentences for hard ideas, parallel structure for lists.
- **Flow & cohesion** — old-info-before-new (each sentence links to the last), signposting, transitions that show the logical relationship.
- **The common mistakes** — burying the contribution, undefined jargon, passive fog, "throat-clearing" intros, results with no interpretation, inconsistent notation.
- **The revision loop** — first draft is *supposed* to be bad; writing = rewriting. Read aloud; cut 10%; get feedback early.
- **Writing well as a non-native English writer** — practical tactics (it's a skill, not a talent).
