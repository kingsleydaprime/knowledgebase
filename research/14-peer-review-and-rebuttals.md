# Peer Review & Rebuttals

**[reference]** · **⏳ outline — deep note in progress** (part of the research-course build).

The gauntlet every paper runs: other experts judge whether it's correct, novel, and significant enough to publish. Understanding review from **both sides** — as an author surviving it and (eventually) as a reviewer doing it — is core research literacy.

## Will cover
- **How review works** — single-blind vs **double-blind** (anonymized both ways), the reviewer → area-chair/editor → decision flow, review criteria (soundness, novelty, significance, clarity), scores and confidence.
- **Reading your reviews without despair** — reviews are often harsh, sometimes wrong, occasionally unfair; separating signal from noise; the emotional reality (rejection is the norm, even for great work).
- **The rebuttal / author response** — the chance to reply before the final decision: how to write one — address every point, be gracious not defensive, provide new evidence/experiments, concede what's fair, clarify misunderstandings without condescension.
- **Revise & resubmit** — major/minor revisions (journals), addressing comments systematically, the response letter.
- **Why papers get rejected** — the common killers: unclear contribution, weak baselines, over-claiming, poor writing, wrong venue — most are [[research/05-methodology-and-experiment-design|preventable]] before submission.
- **Being a good reviewer** — the flip side: fair, specific, constructive, on-time; reviewing teaches you to write better (you see what annoys reviewers).
- **The system's flaws** — reviewer overload, noise/randomness in decisions, bias — and reforms (open review, etc.).
