## The scope note — read this first

**[[INTERVIEW|INTERVIEW.md]] states a principle this bank sits awkwardly against:** *"an interview bank for a subject you haven't practised would be memorisation, not preparation."*

That principle still holds, and this bank exists anyway — **added on request, and honestly labelled.** The material here is derived from the course, not from having sat these interviews. Treat it as **a map of what the round asks**, not as preparation you can substitute for reps.

**What actually prepares you for this round is a finished game.** Games hiring weights the portfolio more heavily than most software hiring — a shipped jam entry with twenty ratings outperforms a perfect answer to Q1 → [[game-development/08-getting-started|getting started]].

## What this round is testing

**Three things, and the weighting surprises people:**

1. **Concrete maths** — vectors and transforms, on a whiteboard, without a library. Cheap to prepare, and candidates routinely fumble it
2. **Performance reasoning under a hard budget** — *"is this CPU- or GPU-bound, and how would you find out?"* is the shape of most technical follow-ups
3. **Evidence you finish things** — the largest single factor, and the one you cannot revise for

**Specialisation matters more than in general software.** Gameplay, engine, graphics, tools, AI and network programming are genuinely different roles with different rounds — this bank covers the shared core → [[foundations/software-engineering/04-the-kinds-of-software-engineering|kinds of software engineering]].
