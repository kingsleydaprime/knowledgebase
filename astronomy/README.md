# Astronomy and Space

The observational science of everything beyond Earth — and an honest treatment of the thing it's most often confused with.

**~9,400 words across 10 notes.** Built August 2026. `[reference]`.

> **The one idea:** **you cannot experiment on your subject.** No star can be heated, no galaxy collided on demand, no Big Bang rerun. Everything is inference from light that has already arrived — which is why spectroscopy, statistics and error bars matter more here than in almost any other science, and why *looking further away* is the only time machine available.

## Why this exists

**Requested for someone else** — not the vault owner's path, and written to stand on its own for a reader arriving with no physics background and no other domain in this vault.

**On astronomy and astrology:** they are not the same thing, and this folder says so plainly. They *were* the same field for two millennia, and that history is genuinely interesting — Ptolemy and Kepler practised both. [[astronomy/09-astrology-honestly|Note 09]] covers what astrology is, what a chart actually contains, what the controlled studies found, and — the useful part — **why it feels accurate anyway**. Those psychological mechanisms apply far beyond astrology and are worth knowing for their own sake.

## The things worth carrying

1. **You cannot experiment on the sky — only observe it.** Everything else follows → [[astronomy/01-what-astronomy-is|01]]
2. **Distance is a time machine.** Looking further away *is* looking earlier → [[astronomy/01-what-astronomy-is|01]]
3. **Seasons are axial tilt, not distance.** Earth is closest to the Sun in January → [[astronomy/02-the-sky-and-how-it-moves|02]]
4. **Precession has shifted the zodiac by about one sign** since it was fixed → [[astronomy/02-the-sky-and-how-it-moves|02]] · [[astronomy/09-astrology-honestly|09]]
5. **Helium was found in the Sun 27 years before it was found on Earth.** That's what spectroscopy does → [[astronomy/03-light-and-instruments|03]]
6. **Space telescopes exist to get above the air**, not to get closer → [[astronomy/03-light-and-instruments|03]]
7. **Mass determines a star's entire life.** More massive means far shorter → [[astronomy/04-stars|04]]
8. **Fusion stops at iron**, and that fact causes supernovae → [[astronomy/04-stars|04]]
9. **Every element heavier than lithium was made in stars** → [[astronomy/04-stars|04]]
10. **The most common exoplanet type has no analogue in our Solar System** → [[astronomy/05-planets-and-solar-systems|05]]
11. **GPS would drift ~10 km per day without general relativity** → [[astronomy/06-gravity-and-relativity|06]]
12. **Space expands; galaxies aren't flying through it.** So there's no centre → [[astronomy/07-cosmology|07]]
13. **~95% of the universe is unidentified**, and the vacuum-energy prediction is off by up to 120 orders of magnitude → [[astronomy/07-cosmology|07]]
14. **Space is not far, it's fast.** ~90% of launch energy is horizontal → [[astronomy/08-spaceflight-and-exploration|08]]
15. **To catch something ahead of you in orbit, slow down** → [[astronomy/08-spaceflight-and-exploration|08]]
16. **Astrology has been tested double-blind and performs at chance** — and the Barnum effect explains why it still feels precise → [[astronomy/09-astrology-honestly|09]]
17. **Binoculars before a telescope.** Aperture beats magnification, always → [[astronomy/10-getting-started|10]]

## The honest note

**`[reference]`, and written by a software engineer, not an astronomer.** Every claim is from standard texts and current mission documentation, and the numbers are as of August 2026 — **exoplanet counts, mission statuses and the Hubble tension will date.**

**Where I'd expect errors:** the cosmology note compresses genuinely contested material, and note 06's treatment of quantum gravity is a summary of a field I can only report on.

**What would close the gap:**

1. **Go outside and find three constellations.** Nothing in this folder substitutes for it
2. **Download a TESS light curve and find a transit yourself** — an afternoon with Astropy, real spacecraft data, and a plot you can explain → [[astronomy/10-getting-started|note 10]]
3. **Classify galaxies on Zooniverse** for an hour. Real data, real contribution
4. **Watch the Moon for one full cycle.** The phases stop being memorised
5. **Read Carroll & Ostlie** if you want the actual physics rather than the map

**What's missing:** the physics done properly (this is descriptive, not derived), galaxies and galaxy evolution as their own note, the interstellar medium, high-energy astrophysics in depth, radio astronomy technique, the history of astronomy as a subject, and exercises.
