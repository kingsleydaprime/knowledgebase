# The Sky and How It Moves

> **[Beginner]** · Coordinates, the motions that produce day, year and seasons, and enough to actually find things.

## The celestial sphere

A useful fiction: pretend everything sits on a sphere around Earth. It's wrong about distance and **exactly right about direction**, which is all you need to point a telescope.

**Horizon coordinates** — where it is *for you, now*:
- **Altitude** — degrees above the horizon (0–90°)
- **Azimuth** — degrees around from north (0–360°)

Simple, and useless for records, because it changes every minute and differs for everyone.

**Equatorial coordinates** — fixed to the sky, the professional system:
- **Right ascension (RA)** — celestial longitude, measured in **hours** (0–24h), because the sky turns 15°/hour
- **Declination (Dec)** — celestial latitude, degrees (−90° to +90°)

Betelgeuse is at RA 5h 55m, Dec +7° 24′ **for everyone, always** — modulo precession, below.

**The projection is literal:** Earth's equator projected outward is the celestial equator; the poles project to the celestial poles. **Polaris sits within a degree of the north celestial pole**, which is why it barely moves and why your latitude equals Polaris's altitude.

## The motions, and what each produces

| Motion | Period | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Earth's rotation** | 23h 56m | Day/night; the sky wheeling westward |
| **Earth's orbit** | 365.26 days | The year; **different constellations by season** |
| **Axial tilt (23.4°)** | — | **The seasons** |
| **Moon's orbit** | 27.3 / 29.5 days | Phases, eclipses, tides |
| **Precession** | ~26,000 years | The pole star changes; **zodiac dates drift** |

**The 23h 56m is not a typo.** That's the **sidereal day** — one rotation relative to the stars. The **solar day** is 24h because Earth also moved along its orbit and must turn ~1° further for the Sun to return to the same place. **Consequence: a given star rises about 4 minutes earlier each night**, which is exactly why the night sky changes through the year.

**Seasons are caused by axial tilt, not distance.** Earth is closest to the Sun in early January — northern winter. Tilt changes the *angle* sunlight strikes the ground and the *hours* of daylight, and both matter more than the ~3% distance variation. **This is the most commonly misheld fact in the subject.**

## The Moon

**Phases are geometry, not shadow.** Half the Moon is always lit; you see a varying fraction of that lit half depending on the Sun–Earth–Moon angle. **Earth's shadow is not involved** — that's a lunar eclipse, which is a different and rarer thing.

**The Moon is tidally locked** — its rotation period equals its orbital period, so one face always points at us. Not a coincidence: tidal friction drove it there, and the same process is slowing Earth's rotation and pushing the Moon away at ~3.8 cm/year.

**Eclipses don't happen monthly** because the Moon's orbit is tilted ~5° to Earth's — so it usually passes above or below. Alignment occurs only near the **nodes**, giving eclipse seasons roughly twice a year.

**A solar eclipse is an extraordinary coincidence:** the Sun is ~400× wider than the Moon and ~400× further away, so they appear the same size. **That coincidence is temporary** — in a few hundred million years the Moon will be too distant for totality.

## Precession, and what it does to the zodiac

Earth's axis wobbles like a spinning top, tracing a circle over **~25,800 years**.

**Consequences:**
- **The pole star changes.** Thuban was the pole star when the pyramids were built; Vega will be in ~12,000 years
- **The equinoxes drift** along the ecliptic
- **The zodiac dates have shifted by roughly one whole sign** since they were fixed ~2,000 years ago

**That last point matters factually:** the Sun is not in the constellation your newspaper horoscope assigns. Someone called "Aries" by the traditional dates has the Sun in Pisces. **Western astrology uses a tropical zodiac tied to the equinox rather than to the actual constellations**, so it is internally consistent about this — but it does mean the sign names no longer describe where the Sun is → [[astronomy/09-astrology-honestly|note 09]].

**Also: there are 13 constellations on the ecliptic.** Ophiuchus is one, and was excluded when the twelve signs were fixed.

## Finding things

**The magnitude scale is backwards, and logarithmic** — a legacy of Hipparchus:
- **Lower = brighter.** Sirius −1.5; Vega 0; naked-eye limit ~6 in dark skies, ~3–4 in a city
- **5 magnitudes = a factor of exactly 100** in brightness

**Apparent** magnitude is how bright it *looks*; **absolute** magnitude is how bright it *would* be at a standard distance. The difference is a distance measurement → [[astronomy/04-stars|note 04]].

**Angular size** — the Moon and the Sun are both ~0.5°. Your fist at arm's length is ~10°, a finger ~1°. **A surprisingly good measuring instrument.**

**Star-hopping** — navigate from bright patterns to fainter targets. The Plough's pointers to Polaris; Orion's belt down to Sirius, up to Aldebaran. **This is the skill that makes a telescope usable**, and no amount of GoTo mounting replaces knowing where you are.
