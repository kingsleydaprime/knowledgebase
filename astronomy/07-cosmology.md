# Cosmology

> **[Intermediate → Advanced]** · The universe as one object — what's established, what's inferred, and what nobody knows.

## The expanding universe

**Hubble, 1929:** distant galaxies are redshifted, and the further away, the more so. **Space itself is expanding.**

**The most important clarification in this note:** galaxies are not flying through space away from us. **Space between them is stretching.** Consequences:

- **There is no centre.** Every observer sees everything receding — the raisin-bread analogy, where every raisin sees every other move away as the dough rises
- **Sufficiently distant galaxies recede faster than light**, and this violates nothing. Special relativity limits motion *through* space; the expansion *of* space has no such limit
- **Cosmological redshift is not a Doppler shift.** The light's wavelength stretched *in transit* → [[astronomy/03-light-and-instruments|note 03]]

**The Hubble constant** $H_0$ sets the rate — currently ~70 km/s/Mpc, and see the tension below.

## The Big Bang

**What the theory says:** the universe was hotter and denser in the past and has been expanding and cooling for **13.8 billion years**.

**What it does not say:** that there was an explosion at a point in pre-existing space, or what happened "before". **The theory describes the evolution of the universe from a very early hot dense state — not its origin.** Conflating those is the most common misunderstanding of the subject.

**The three pillars of evidence**, and it's worth knowing why cosmologists find them convincing:

**1. Hubble expansion.** Run it backwards and everything was together.

**2. The cosmic microwave background.** ~380,000 years in, the universe cooled enough for atoms to form and became transparent. **That light is still arriving, redshifted to microwaves at 2.7 K.** Predicted before it was found; discovered accidentally in 1965. **Its tiny temperature fluctuations — one part in 100,000 — are the seeds of every galaxy**, and mapping them (COBE, WMAP, Planck) is how most cosmological parameters were measured.

**3. Primordial abundances.** Big Bang nucleosynthesis predicts ~75% hydrogen, ~25% helium and traces of lithium and deuterium. **Observed abundances match** across independent measurements.

**Together these are why the Big Bang model is not seriously contested.** They're independent, quantitative, and were substantially predictive rather than fitted.

## Inflation

**A proposed period of exponential expansion in the first ~10⁻³² seconds**, invented to solve three problems: why the universe looks the same in opposite directions that were never in causal contact (**horizon**), why it's so close to geometrically flat (**flatness**), and why we see no magnetic monopoles.

**It also predicted the CMB fluctuation spectrum**, which was then measured and matched.

**Its status, honestly:** widely accepted, and **not confirmed at the level the three pillars are.** There is no consensus on the mechanism, the models are flexible enough to accommodate a range of outcomes, and some cosmologists argue it is under-constrained. **Detecting primordial gravitational waves (B-mode polarisation) would be strong evidence; none has been confirmed** — the 2014 BICEP2 claim turned out to be galactic dust, which is a good case study in how the field self-corrects → [[research/README|research]].

## The dark sector

**The uncomfortable core of modern cosmology: ~95% of the universe's content is unidentified.**

| | Share | What we know |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary matter** | ~5% | Everything ever observed directly |
| **Dark matter** | ~27% | Has gravity, doesn't interact with light |
| **Dark energy** | ~68% | Drives accelerating expansion |

**Dark matter** — inferred from galaxy rotation curves (outer stars orbit too fast), gravitational lensing, cluster dynamics, and the CMB's structure. **The Bullet Cluster is the strongest single piece of evidence**: two colliding clusters where lensing shows the mass separated from the visible gas, which is very hard to explain by modifying gravity.

**No direct detection despite decades of increasingly sensitive experiments.** WIMPs were the leading candidate and the parameter space is being closed; axions and primordial black holes remain in play.

**Dark energy** — in 1998 two teams measuring distant supernovae found the expansion **accelerating**, not slowing. Nobel Prize 2011. The simplest explanation is a **cosmological constant** — an intrinsic energy of space.

**And the theoretical situation is embarrassing:** quantum field theory's naive estimate of vacuum energy exceeds the observed value by up to **120 orders of magnitude**. **This is arguably the worst prediction in the history of physics**, and it is unresolved.

## The Hubble tension

**A live, unresolved disagreement worth knowing because it shows the field working.**

Two independent ways to measure $H_0$ give different answers:
- **Early-universe** (CMB + the standard model) → ~67 km/s/Mpc
- **Late-universe** (distance ladder: Cepheids → supernovae) → ~73 km/s/Mpc

**The error bars no longer overlap.** Either one measurement has an unrecognised systematic, or **the standard cosmological model is incomplete.**

**Neither side has found the error, and both have been checked repeatedly by independent groups.** This is what an actual open problem looks like — not a scandal, and not settled.

## The fate of the universe

Determined by dark energy's behaviour, which is not well constrained:

- **Heat death** — expansion continues, stars burn out, everything cools toward equilibrium. **The default expectation**
- **Big Rip** — if dark energy strengthens, expansion eventually tears apart galaxies, then atoms
- **Big Crunch** — recollapse. Disfavoured by the observed acceleration

**Recent survey results (DESI) hint that dark energy may not be constant**, which would matter enormously. **Treat that as interesting and unconfirmed.**
