# Gravity and Relativity

> **[Intermediate → Advanced]** · Newton, Einstein, and the objects where the difference stops being academic.

## Newton, and where he still works

$$F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

**Newtonian gravity is astonishingly good.** It got humans to the Moon, runs every spacecraft trajectory in the Solar System, and predicted Neptune's existence from Uranus's orbital deviations before anyone saw it.

**It is also, strictly, wrong** — and the first place it visibly failed was Mercury.

## General relativity

Einstein's reframing: **gravity is not a force. Mass and energy curve spacetime, and objects follow the straightest available paths through that curved geometry.**

Wheeler's summary is the one to keep: *spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve.*

**The predictions, and how each was confirmed:**

**Mercury's perihelion precession** — an anomalous 43 arcseconds per century that Newton couldn't explain. GR gave exactly that, with no free parameters. **A postdiction, and the more impressive for it.**

**Light bending** — measured during the 1919 eclipse. Made Einstein famous overnight, and is now a working tool: **gravitational lensing** magnifies distant galaxies and maps invisible mass.

**Gravitational time dilation** — clocks run slower in stronger gravity. **This is not a curiosity: GPS satellites gain ~38 microseconds per day relative to ground clocks**, and uncorrected that would accumulate ~10 km of positioning error daily. **Every phone with satellite navigation depends on GR being right.**

**Gravitational redshift** — light climbing out of a well loses energy → [[astronomy/03-light-and-instruments|note 03]].

**Gravitational waves** — predicted 1916, **detected 2015** by LIGO from two merging black holes. The signal moved the detector mirrors by a fraction of a proton's width. It opened an entirely new observational channel.

## Black holes

**A region where escape velocity exceeds the speed of light.** Not a hole and not a vacuum cleaner — **a black hole with the Sun's mass would leave Earth's orbit unchanged.**

**The parts:**
- **Event horizon** — the boundary of no return. At the Schwarzschild radius, $r_s = 2GM/c^2$. **Not a physical surface**; nothing special happens locally as you cross
- **Singularity** — where GR predicts infinite density, which is understood as **the theory breaking down**, not as a physical fact. Resolving it needs quantum gravity, which doesn't exist yet
- **Accretion disc** — infalling matter, heated by friction to millions of degrees. **This is what we actually observe**; the hole itself emits nothing

**The types:**
- **Stellar-mass** (~5–100 M☉) — from core collapse → [[astronomy/04-stars|note 04]]
- **Supermassive** (10⁶–10¹⁰ M☉) — at galactic centres, including **Sagittarius A\*** at ours. How they grew so large so early is an open problem
- **Intermediate-mass** — long elusive, now with growing evidence

**The evidence is direct.** Stars orbiting Sagittarius A* have been tracked for decades, giving a mass of ~4 million M☉ in a region smaller than Mercury's orbit — that observation won the 2020 Nobel Prize. And the **Event Horizon Telescope imaged the shadow** of M87* in 2019 and Sgr A* in 2022 → [[astronomy/03-light-and-instruments|interferometry]].

**Hawking radiation** — quantum effects near the horizon should make black holes evaporate, slowly. **A stellar-mass black hole would take ~10⁶⁷ years**, so it is entirely unobserved and remains theoretical. It also generates the **information paradox**, which is unresolved and is one of the sharpest known conflicts between GR and quantum mechanics.

## Neutron stars

**What's left when a core collapses but stops short of a black hole.** Roughly 1.4–2 M☉ compressed into ~20 km.

**The numbers are hard to take seriously and are correct:** a teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh about a billion tonnes. Surface gravity ~10¹¹ times Earth's. Magnetic fields up to 10¹¹ tesla.

**Pulsars** are rapidly rotating neutron stars with beamed emission sweeping past us — **natural clocks**, some stable enough to rival atomic clocks. The first was briefly labelled "LGM-1" for *little green men* before the rotation explanation was found.

**They are also physics laboratories we cannot build.** Neutron-star matter is denser than an atomic nucleus, and its equation of state is genuinely unknown — constrained by measuring masses and radii, and now by gravitational waves from mergers.

## Where GR is still open

**Two genuinely unsolved problems**, and both are honest:

**Quantum gravity.** GR describes the very large; quantum mechanics the very small. **They are incompatible where both matter** — singularities, the first instant of the universe. String theory and loop quantum gravity are candidates; **neither has experimental support.**

**Dark matter and dark energy** → [[astronomy/07-cosmology|note 07]]. Galaxies rotate as though they contain far more mass than we see, and the expansion is accelerating. **Together these are ~95% of the universe's content, and we don't know what either is.**

**Modified gravity (MOND and successors)** proposes changing the law instead of adding matter. It fits galaxy rotation curves remarkably well and **fails on galaxy clusters and the CMB**, which is why most of the field favours dark matter — while acknowledging that "most of the universe is something we've never detected" is not a comfortable position.
