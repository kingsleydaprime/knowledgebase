# Phishing and Scams

**[Beginner]** — how people actually lose money, and the two habits that stop nearly all of it.

## The kid version first

Almost nothing that reaches you is a technical attack. **It's a message designed to make you act before you think.**

The whole craft is manufactured urgency: *your account will be closed*, *your parcel is held*, *there's suspicious activity*. Panic is the exploit. Everything else is decoration.

**Two habits stop most of it:**
1. **Never act from a link in a message.** Open the app or type the address yourself.
2. **Verify urgency through a channel you chose** — a number you looked up, not the one in the message.

## The tells

**Urgency and threat.** *"Within 24 hours or your account will be suspended."* **Real organisations don't work this way.** Manufactured time pressure exists to prevent you thinking, and it's the single most reliable indicator.

**An unexpected link or attachment**, even from someone you know — their account may be the one that's compromised.

**A request that breaks procedure.** *"I'm in a meeting, can you just pay this invoice quickly?"* — the boss who never texts, texting.

**Slightly wrong details.** `paypa1.com`, `micros0ft-support.net`, a display name that doesn't match the address behind it. **Hover over links before clicking; on mobile, long-press to preview.**

**Wrong tone or context.** A bank that suddenly uses your first name oddly, or a service you don't use.

**Two old tells that no longer work:** bad spelling and poor design. **AI has removed both.** Modern phishing is well written and pixel-perfect, so *"it looked professional"* is no longer evidence of anything.

## The varieties

**Bulk email phishing** — millions of copies. Fake login pages for Microsoft 365, banks, delivery firms.

**Spear phishing** — targeted, researched, using real details about you or your workplace. Far more convincing, far fewer copies.

**Smishing (text) and vishing (voice).** *"Your parcel couldn't be delivered — pay £1.99."* Voice is now the dangerous one: **AI voice cloning** means a distressed relative's voice can be faked from a few seconds of social media audio.

> **Agree a family code word.** A word only your family knows, for verifying an urgent call. It costs one conversation and defeats voice cloning entirely.

**Business email compromise (BEC)** — the most expensive category in the world by value. A supplier emails new bank details for an outstanding invoice. The email is real, from a compromised account, and the details are the attacker's. **Verify any change of payment details by phone, on a number you already had.**

**Tech support scams** — a pop-up or call about a virus, leading to remote-access software and "refund" theatre. **Microsoft and Apple will never call you.**

**Romance and investment fraud ("pig butchering")** — weeks or months of genuine-feeling relationship, then an investment opportunity on a fake platform showing fake gains. Small withdrawals succeed; large ones require "fees". **Often operated from trafficked-labour compounds** — organised crime, not opportunists. **The largest category by money lost**, and the victims are not foolish; they were groomed for months.

**Recovery scams** — someone who "can get your lost money back", targeting people already defrauded. **Being scammed once puts you on a list.**

**Marketplace and rental scams** — the deposit for a flat you can't view, the item that never ships.

## The rules

**1. Never use the link. Ever.**
Not because you can't spot fakes, but because you don't have to. Open your banking app. Type the address. Use your bookmark. **Nothing legitimate is lost by arriving under your own steam.**

**2. Verify out of band.**
Urgent request about money or credentials → contact them on a number or address **you already had**. Never the contact details in the message. If it's genuine, a five-minute delay costs nothing.

**3. Nobody legitimate asks for these:**
- Your password
- A code that was sent to you → [[cybersecurity/10-protecting-yourself/03-two-factor-and-passkeys|two-factor]]
- Remote access to your computer
- Payment in gift cards, crypto, or bank transfer to "keep your money safe"
- A crypto seed phrase → [[web3/08-the-honest-assessment/02-scams-rugs-and-the-fraud-surface|the crypto fraud surface]]

**4. Slow down when you feel urgency.** The feeling of needing to act *right now* is the attack working. **Treat it as the alarm itself.**

**5. Use a password manager as a phishing detector.** It fills credentials based on the domain — **so if it doesn't offer to fill, that's not the real site.** A genuinely useful side effect.

## If you clicked

Don't panic, and don't stay quiet — **shame is what turns a small incident into a large one.**

1. **Entered a password?** Change it immediately, everywhere you reused it, and sign out all sessions → [[cybersecurity/10-protecting-yourself/08-when-it-goes-wrong|when it goes wrong]]
2. **Entered card details?** Call your bank, freeze the card
3. **Installed something or gave remote access?** Disconnect from the network, and treat the device as compromised — **a wipe and reinstall is the only reliable fix**
4. **Sent money?** Contact your bank **immediately** — same-day recall sometimes works. Report it (Action Fraud in the UK, IC3 in the US, your local equivalent)
5. **At work?** Tell IT **now**. They have seen it before and would far rather hear it early. **Concealment is the thing that causes real damage**

## If you're helping someone else

Most people reading this will at some point be the person a parent or friend calls.

- **Don't lead with "how did you fall for that".** They'll stop telling you things, and that's worse than any single scam
- **Set the six basics up *for* them** → [[cybersecurity/10-protecting-yourself/01-your-actual-threat-model|the six things]]. Doing beats explaining
- **Give them one rule they'll remember:** *"If it's urgent and about money, hang up and call me."*
- **Agree the family code word**
- Consider being a trusted contact on their accounts

## Key insight

**Phishing targets the person, not the computer** — so no amount of software fixes it, and no amount of technical skill makes you immune. What works is procedural: never act from a link, and verify anything urgent through a channel you chose yourself. Those two habits require no expertise and defeat the overwhelming majority of what will ever be aimed at you.
